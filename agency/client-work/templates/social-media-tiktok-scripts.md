### The 5 Script Types (One of Each)

Each weekly batch must include exactly one of each type:

1. **Trending Format Adapted to Brand** — Take a current trending TikTok format (POV, "things that just make sense," "tell me without telling me," green screen rant, split screen, "put a finger down," ranking tier list, etc.) and adapt it to the brand. The format should be recognizable to TikTok users but the content should be brand-relevant. Reference the trend explicitly so the content team knows what format to film in.

2. **Product Demo / Tutorial** — Show the product in action. This should be educational and useful, not a commercial. Think "how to" or "watch me use this" or "the right way to [do something]." The product should be the star but the value should come from the knowledge shared.

3. **"Day in the Life" / Behind the Scenes** — Take viewers behind the curtain. This could be the founder's morning routine, a day at the office/warehouse, how products are made, packing orders, or any authentic glimpse into the real people behind the brand. Should feel unfiltered and personal.

4. **Response to Comment / FAQ** — Film as if responding to a specific comment or common question. Use the TikTok "reply to comment" format or the "I get asked this all the time" opener. Address a real objection, misconception, or frequently asked question about the product or industry.

5. **Storytelling / Brand Narrative** — Tell a compelling story about the brand, founder, mission, or a customer experience. This should have a clear narrative arc: setup, tension/conflict, resolution. It should make people feel something — surprise, inspiration, humor, or connection.

### Writing Rules You Must Follow

1. **Write exactly how people talk on TikTok.** Not how brands talk. Not how copywriters talk. How real people on TikTok talk. Short sentences. Conversational fragments. Verbal filler when it feels natural ("like," "literally," "okay so"). Personality-forward. If you read the script out loud and it sounds like an ad, rewrite it.

2. **The hook is everything.** The first 1-3 seconds determine whether someone watches or scrolls. Every hook must create an open loop, challenge an assumption, state something surprising, or trigger curiosity. The viewer must feel like they NEED to keep watching. Test each hook by asking: "Would I stop scrolling for this?"

3. **Include precise timing cues.** Every script must have timestamp markers for every beat. The viewer should never go more than 3-5 seconds without something new happening on screen — a new shot, a text overlay change, a visual transition, or a new piece of information.

4. **Pacing must be TikTok-fast.** TikTok attention spans are brutal. Get to the point immediately. No wind-ups, no "before I start let me give you some context." Front-load the most interesting information. Every second must earn the next second.

5. **Text overlays are critical.** Most people watch TikTok without sound initially. The text overlays must tell the story independently of the audio. They should be punchy, scan-able, and reinforce the key moments. Timestamp every text overlay.

6. **Visual direction must be shot-by-shot.** Do not give vague directions. For each timestamp, describe exactly what the camera shows: angle, framing, movement, what the person is doing, what products or props are visible, lighting, and any transitions. A videographer should be able to film this without asking any questions.

7. **CTAs must feel natural.** Never end with "link in bio" as the last thing said — it kills energy. Wrap the CTA into the content naturally. The best CTAs on TikTok feel like recommendations from a friend, not a sales pitch.

8. **Reference specific trending formats.** When using a trending format, name it explicitly so the content team knows what to reference. Describe how the format works so they can see examples and adapt their filming style.

9. **Every script must be filmable on a phone.** No scripts that require professional equipment, complex editing, or special effects. Everything should be achievable with a smartphone, natural lighting, and basic editing tools (CapCut, in-app editor).

10. **Sound suggestions should be strategic.** For each script, suggest either a trending sound (describe the type since trends change), original audio, or a specific music vibe. Explain why the sound choice supports the content.

## Quality Checks Before Sending

Run through every single check before delivering to the client:

1. **Hook test (the 1.5-second rule):** For each script, read only the hook. Would you personally stop scrolling? If the hook sounds like something a brand would say rather than something a person would say, rewrite it. Every hook must create an open loop or trigger genuine curiosity.

2. **Read-aloud test:** Read every script out loud at normal speaking speed. Does it sound natural? Does the timing work? If any line sounds like written copy rather than spoken words, rewrite it to sound conversational. Time yourself — if any segment runs over its timestamp, trim it.

3. **Sound-off test:** Read only the text overlays in sequence, ignoring all spoken audio. Do the text overlays alone tell a compelling, understandable story? If someone watched with sound off, would they still get the point and want to watch to the end?

4. **TikTok native check:** Would this video feel at home on a For You page? Or would it feel like an ad that got dropped into the feed? If it feels like an ad, add more personality, more imperfection, more human moments. The best TikTok brand content is barely distinguishable from creator content.

5. **Filmability check:** Can every shot be filmed with a smartphone by one person (or one person plus one helper)? Are there any shots that require special equipment, multiple camera angles simultaneously, or complex editing? If so, simplify.

6. **Words-to-avoid scan:** Search every script — including spoken words, text overlays, and captions — for any word or phrase from the client's "Words to Avoid" list. Remove and replace any that appear.

7. **CTA natural integration:** Check that no script ends with a hard sell or an awkward "link in bio." The CTA should flow naturally from the content and feel like a genuine recommendation, not a commercial break.

### Script 1: "Ingredients I'd Never Put On My Face (As a Chemist)"

**Format Type:** Trending Format Adapted to Brand
**Trending Format Reference:** "Green screen ranking / reaction" format — creator stands in front of a green screen showing images or lists and gives their hot takes. This format is evergreen on SkinTok and drives massive comment debate. Film with the creator on the left third of frame, green screen image on the right two-thirds.
**Estimated Length:** 38 seconds
**Filming Difficulty:** Easy — just needs green screen feature in TikTok app and product images

**HOOK [0:00-0:02]**
- What's said: "I spent twelve years making skincare for big brands, and there are ingredients I will never put on my face."
- What's shown: Priya looking directly at camera, medium shot from chest up, natural lighting from a window. She's holding up three fingers. Background is clean and minimal — a white or light wall.
- Text overlay: "Cosmetic chemist of 12 years"

**BODY [0:02-0:08]**
- What's said: "Number one — fragrance. And I mean any fragrance. Even the ones labeled 'natural fragrance' or 'essential oil blend.' Your skin does not need to smell like a candle. All fragrance is a potential irritant. I formulated with it for years because brands told me to. Never again."
- What's shown: Green screen switches to a close-up image of an ingredient list with "fragrance" circled in red. Priya gestures at it. Cut to close-up of her face looking unimpressed.
- Text overlay: [0:02] "1. FRAGRANCE" [0:05] "yes, even 'natural' fragrance"

**[0:08-0:15]**
- What's said: "Number two — denatured alcohol. It's in so many toners and essences. It gives you that clean tight feeling. You know what that feeling actually is? Your skin barrier being stripped. That tightness is damage."
- What's shown: Green screen shows an ingredient list with "alcohol denat." highlighted. Then cut to Priya shaking her head. She picks up a Bare Route serum bottle and holds it casually, not showing it to camera yet — just holding it like a prop.
- Text overlay: [0:08] "2. DENATURED ALCOHOL" [0:11] "that 'tight clean' feeling = barrier damage"

**[0:15-0:23]**
- What's said: "Number three — and this one is going to be controversial — most forms of vitamin C in serums. I know, I know. But here's the thing. L-ascorbic acid is incredibly unstable. By the time most vitamin C serums get to your face, they've already oxidized. You're putting rusty vitamin C on your skin. There are better ways to deliver antioxidants."
- What's shown: Green screen shows a yellowed/oxidized vitamin C serum bottle (generic, no brand shown). Priya makes a face. Then she holds up her hands in a "hear me out" gesture as she anticipates pushback.
- Text overlay: [0:15] "3. MOST VITAMIN C SERUMS" [0:18] "(controversial but hear me out)" [0:20] "L-ascorbic acid oxidizes before it hits your face"

**[0:23-0:32]**

- What's shown: Green screen off. Cut to Priya's bathroom counter. She holds up each of the three Bare Route products one at a time, showing the short ingredient list on the back of each. Close-up of the ingredient list showing how short it is. Back to face, genuine expression.
- Text overlay: [0:23] "so I made my own line" [0:26] "5 ingredients or fewer" [0:29] "nothing your skin doesn't need"

**CTA/CLOSER [0:32-0:38]**
- What's said: "If you've been layering ten products and your skin still freaks out, maybe it's not your skin. Maybe it's the ten products. I'll drop the code in comments."
- What's shown: Close up on Priya's face, slightly amused smile. She raises one eyebrow. Quick cut to all three products lined up neatly on a clean counter. End on Priya giving a small, knowing nod to camera.
- Text overlay: [0:32] "if your routine has 10 steps and your skin still freaks out..." [0:35] "it's not your skin"

**Sound Suggestion:** Original audio — Priya's voice is the main attraction here. No background music needed, or very subtle lo-fi beat underneath at low volume. Original audio performs well for educational content on SkinTok because it gets saved and stitched. Use trending sounds only if there's one that fits the ranking/reaction format currently.

**Caption for Post:**
12 years of formulating skincare for big brands and these are the ingredients I refuse to use. The vitamin C one is going to start a war in the comments and I'm ready. #skincare #skintok #cosmeticchemist #ingredients #skinbarrier #bareroute #skincareroutine #simpleskincare

**Why This Works:**
The green screen ranking format is one of the most reliable engagement drivers on SkinTok because it invites debate. Leading with "cosmetic chemist of 12 years" establishes immediate authority and makes people stop scrolling. The vitamin C take is intentionally controversial, which will drive comments — and TikTok's algorithm prioritizes comment velocity. The product mention feels earned because it comes after genuine value, not before.

### Script 4: "Why Your 10-Step Routine Is Breaking You Out"
