# Email Welcome Sequence (5-Email Series)

## Quick Reference

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **What it produces** | A complete 5-email welcome sequence for DTC brand email subscribers, including subject lines, preview text, body copy, CTAs, and internal strategy notes |
| **Turnaround** | 45-60 minutes per sequence |
| **Quality bar** | Production-ready copy that can be loaded directly into Klaviyo, Omnisend, or any ESP with minimal formatting adjustments |
| **Best for** | New DTC clients onboarding their email program, brands launching a new list-building campaign, clients migrating ESPs who need fresh welcome flows |

### Sequence Structure and Requirements

Write exactly 5 emails following this structure:

**Email 1 — Welcome + Brand Story + Discount (Send: Immediately)**
- Purpose: Deliver on the signup promise. Introduce the brand personality in 30 seconds. Make the subscriber glad they signed up.
- Body length: 150-250 words
- Must include: The discount code or lead magnet promised at signup, a 2-3 sentence brand story hook, one clear CTA to shop
- Emotional target: Excitement + belonging ("you're in the right place")

**Email 2 — Social Proof + Bestsellers (Send: Day 2)**
- Purpose: Build trust through other people's words. Reduce purchase anxiety by showing what's popular.
- Body length: 150-200 words
- Must include: 2-3 real-sounding customer review snippets (write these as realistic examples), mention of top 3 bestselling products by name, UGC-style reference (e.g., "tagged us 5,000+ times on Instagram")
- Emotional target: Trust + FOMO ("everyone loves this")

**Email 3 — Founder Story / Why We Exist (Send: Day 4)**
- Purpose: Create emotional connection. Make the brand feel human, not corporate. Give subscribers a reason to care.
- Body length: 200-300 words
- Must include: A compelling founder origin story, the "moment of frustration" that led to creating the brand, brand values stated through story (not a bullet list), a vulnerable or honest moment
- Emotional target: Connection + alignment ("these are my people")

**Email 4 — Education / Value (Send: Day 7)**
- Purpose: Provide genuine value. Show expertise. Help the subscriber before asking for money.
- Body length: 150-250 words
- Must include: A useful tip, how-to, or piece of content related to the product, specific product mention woven naturally into the advice, content that would be valuable even if they never buy
- Emotional target: Gratitude + authority ("this brand actually knows their stuff")

**Email 5 — Urgency / Conversion (Send: Day 10)**
- Purpose: Close the loop. Create genuine urgency. Convert subscribers who have been engaged but haven't purchased.
- Body length: 100-200 words
- Must include: Reminder of discount/offer (if applicable), a specific deadline or scarcity element, a direct ask to purchase, reassurance (guarantee, free returns, etc.)
- Emotional target: Now-or-never + confidence ("I should just do this")

### Writing Rules

1. **Paragraphs:** Maximum 2-3 sentences per paragraph. White space is your friend. These are read on phones.
2. **First line:** The opening line of every email must hook within 6 words. Front-load the interesting part.
3. **Voice consistency:** Every email must sound like it was written by the same person. Match the brand voice variables exactly.
4. **Product specificity:** Mention actual product names (invent realistic ones if needed). Never say "our products" — say "our Midnight Crunch bars" or "the Rosemary Sea Salt blend."
5. **Subject lines:** Use proven DTC formulas: curiosity gap, personal ("your"), benefit-first, emoji sparingly (max 1), pattern interrupt. Never use ALL CAPS for full words. Never use clickbait that the email doesn't deliver on.
6. **Conversation flow:** The 5 emails should feel like a conversation arc: introduction, proof, heart, value, action. Reference previous emails lightly (e.g., "remember when I said..." in email 3).
7. **CTA clarity:** One primary CTA per email. Button text should be specific (not "Shop Now" — try "Grab Your Starter Pack" or "See What 10,000 Snackers Love").
8. **Dynamic tokens:** Use {{first_name}} where a personalized greeting makes sense — but not in every email. Do not overuse personalization tokens.
9. **Unsubscribe psychology:** Never beg people to stay. The tone of the sequence should make unsubscribing feel like missing out, not escaping.
10. **Discount handling:** If a discount code is part of the welcome offer, mention it in Email 1 (deliver the promise), reference it casually in Email 3 or 4, and use it as urgency in Email 5. Do not mention the discount in every single email.

## Output Format

The output should be structured exactly as follows:

- **Total emails:** 5
- **Per email:** Subject lines (3), preview text (1), body copy (with formatting cues), CTA button text (1), internal notes (4 sub-items)
- **Word counts per email body:** Email 1: 150-250 | Email 2: 150-200 | Email 3: 200-300 | Email 4: 150-250 | Email 5: 100-200
- **Total deliverable word count:** Approximately 1,500-2,500 words of copy, plus internal notes
- **Formatting:** Markdown with clear headers, code blocks for subject lines, and bracketed formatting cues in body copy
- **Naming convention:** Each email should have a descriptive title (e.g., "Email 1: Welcome to the Pack")

---

## Quality Checks Before Sending

Run through every item before delivering to the client:

1. **Voice match:** Read all 5 emails aloud. Do they sound like the same brand? Do they match the tone scale? If the client is a "7" on tone, email 3 shouldn't suddenly read like a "3."
2. **Subject line variety:** Are all 15 subject lines genuinely different in approach? No two should use the same formula (e.g., if two start with "Your..." — rewrite one).
3. **Word count compliance:** Check each email body against its target range. Email 2 should NOT be 300 words. Email 5 should NOT be 75 words. Use a word counter.
4. **CTA specificity:** Every CTA button should be specific to the email's content. If you see "Shop Now" or "Learn More" anywhere, rewrite it. Good CTAs: "Try the Starter Kit," "Read Their Reviews," "Claim Your 15% Off."
5. **Discount continuity:** If a discount code is promised, confirm it appears in Email 1, is referenced in the middle of the sequence, and has urgency in Email 5. The code itself should be consistent across all mentions.
6. **Product names:** Confirm that specific product names appear in at least 3 of the 5 emails. No email should only say "our products" generically.
7. **Mobile readability:** No paragraph should be longer than 3 sentences. No sentence should be longer than 25 words. Check for wall-of-text sections and break them up.

- Price range: $8-24
