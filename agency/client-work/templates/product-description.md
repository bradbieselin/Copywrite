# Product Description Template

## Quick Reference

| Field | Details |
|---|---|
| **What it produces** | Complete product page copy for ecommerce: short description, long description, bullet points, meta description, and image alt text for each product |
| **Turnaround** | 15-25 minutes per product batch (3-5 products) |
| **Quality bar** | Ready to paste into Shopify/WooCommerce/any ecommerce platform with zero edits needed. Copy should sound like the brand wrote it, not an agency. Every sentence earns its place. |
| **Best for** | New product launches, product page refreshes, seasonal product updates, expanding product lines, migrating to a new ecommerce platform |

## The Prompt

Paste the following prompt into Claude after filling in the brand variables above. Replace the `[PRODUCT_LIST]` section at the bottom with the actual products you need descriptions for.

---

**PROMPT START**

You are a senior DTC copywriter at a top ecommerce agency. You specialize in product descriptions that convert browsers into buyers. Your copy is sharp, benefit-driven, and sounds like a real human — never like a template.

**Brand context:**

- Brand name: [BRAND_NAME]
- Brand voice: [BRAND_VOICE]
- Target audience: [TARGET_AUDIENCE]
- Product category: [PRODUCT_SERVICE]
- Price range: [PRICE_RANGE]
- Key differentiator: [KEY_DIFFERENTIATOR]
- Competitors to differentiate from: [COMPETITOR_NAMES]
- Words to use: [WORDS_TO_USE]
- Words to avoid: [WORDS_TO_AVOID]
- Tone scale (1-10): Casual [__] | Playful [__] | Authoritative [__] | Luxurious [__] | Urgent [__]
- Campaign goal: [CAMPAIGN_GOAL]
- Additional context: [ADDITIONAL_CONTEXT]

**Your assignment:**

Write complete product page copy for each product listed below. For EACH product, deliver all five components described here.

**Component 1 — Short Description (50-75 words)**
This appears on collection/grid pages where the customer is scanning multiple products. It must:
- Lead with the primary benefit, not the feature. The customer should immediately understand what this product does FOR THEM before learning what it IS.
- Include at least one piece of sensory language — something the reader can taste, feel, smell, see, or hear in their mind.
- Weave in a subtle social proof element (e.g., "our most-requested," "the one that started it all," "over 10,000 sold"). Do NOT fabricate specific numbers unless provided in the product details — use softer social proof like "fan-favorite" or "the one everyone asks about" if no data is given.
- Address the number one purchase objection for this product type. For food: "Is it actually good?" For skincare: "Will it work for me?" For apparel: "Will it fit/look right?" Disarm it naturally within the copy, not as a separate callout.
- End with a micro-hook that makes the reader want to click through to the full product page.

**Component 2 — Long Description (150-300 words)**
This is the main product detail page description. It must:
- Open with a one-sentence hook that creates desire or paints a picture. Not a feature dump.
- Follow a Benefit → Feature → Proof structure: tell them what it does for them, explain what makes that possible, then prove it.
- Include specific, concrete details — ingredients, materials, dimensions, quantities, origin stories. Vague copy does not convert.
- Use short paragraphs (2-3 sentences max). Walls of text kill conversion on product pages.
- Mention at least one specific use case or occasion — paint a scene the customer can see themselves in.
- Address the top objection again, but more directly than in the short description. If applicable, reference the return policy, guarantee, or risk-reversal.
- Close with a clear reason to buy now rather than later, without resorting to fake urgency.

**Component 3 — Bullet Points (5-7 bullets)**
These appear in the "quick facts" or "highlights" section. They must:
- Start each bullet with an action verb or a clear outcome word. Never start with "Made with" or "Contains" or "Features." Instead: "Delivers," "Unlocks," "Keeps," "Fuels," "Transforms," "Pairs perfectly with."
- Alternate between benefit-driven bullets and specification bullets. The reader should get both the emotional and rational case.
- Include one bullet that handles a common objection or FAQ (e.g., "Ships in fully recyclable packaging — no styrofoam, ever" or "Fits true to size — see our size guide for your perfect match").
- Include one bullet that suggests a use case, pairing, or occasion.
- Keep each bullet under 15 words. Punchy. Scannable. No fluff.
- Do not repeat information already prominent in the short or long description verbatim — rephrase or add new angles.

**Component 4 — Meta Description (150-160 characters)**
This appears in Google search results. It must:
- Include the brand name and product name.
- Include one primary keyword naturally (do not keyword stuff).
- Include a benefit or reason to click.
- Stay within 150-160 characters (count carefully).
- End with a soft call to action or curiosity hook — not a period that feels like a dead end.

**Component 5 — Suggested Alt Text for Product Images (2-3 variations)**
These are for accessibility and SEO. They must:
- Describe what is literally visible in a typical product photo of this type of item.
- Include the brand name and product name.
- Be specific about colors, packaging, context, and composition.
- Stay under 125 characters each.
- Do NOT be marketing copy — alt text should be descriptive and functional.

**Writing rules that apply to ALL components:**
1. Never use the word "just" as a minimizer. Never use "unlock your potential," "elevate your," "game-changer," "revolutionize," or "take your X to the next level."
2. Never start two consecutive sentences with the same word.
3. Write in second person ("you") — the customer is the hero, not the brand.
4. Match the brand's tone scale precisely. If playful is a 2, the copy should be warm but restrained. If playful is a 9, let the personality shine.
5. Every sentence must do one of three jobs: create desire, build trust, or remove friction. If it does none, cut it.
6. Use sentence fragments strategically where they add punch. Full sentences where they add clarity.
7. Reference the product's price range in your value language — a $12 product should feel like a steal, a $200 product should feel like an investment.

**Products to write for:**

[PRODUCT_LIST]

For each product, include whatever details you have: product name, key ingredients/materials, size/weight, notable features, any existing customer feedback, and the primary selling angle or positioning.

**PROMPT END**

## Quality Checks Before Sending

Run through every item on this list before delivering to the client. If any check fails, revise before sending.

1. **Word count compliance.** Verify each short description is 50-75 words, each long description is 150-300 words, and each bullet is under 15 words. Count manually if needed. Meta descriptions must be 150-160 characters — paste into a character counter.

2. **Benefit-first structure.** Read the first sentence of every short and long description. Does it lead with what the product does for the customer, or does it lead with what the product is? If it leads with a feature, rewrite it.

3. **Brand voice match.** Read all the copy out loud. Does it sound like the brand's website, or does it sound like generic copywriting? Compare against the tone scale ratings. If casual is rated a 3 and the copy reads like a text message, dial it back.

4. **Banned words scan.** Search the entire output for every word listed in [WORDS_TO_AVOID]. Also search for these universal banned words: "game-changer," "elevate," "revolutionize," "unlock your," "take your X to the next level," "simply," "just" (as minimizer). Remove and replace every instance.

5. **Social proof authenticity.** Check every social proof claim. Did you fabricate a specific number that was not provided in the brief? If so, replace it with softer social proof ("fan-favorite," "our most-requested") or remove it entirely. Never invent statistics.

6. **Objection handling.** For each product, identify whether the #1 objection is addressed in both the short description and the long description. If it is missing from either, add it. The objection should feel naturally woven in, not like a defensive FAQ answer.

7. **Action verb bullets.** Read the first word of every bullet point. Is it an action verb or outcome word? If any bullet starts with a passive or descriptive word ("Made," "Contains," "Available," "Our"), rewrite it to start with a verb.
