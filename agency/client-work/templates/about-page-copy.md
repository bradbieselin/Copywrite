# About Page Copy Template

## Quick Reference

| Field | Details |
|---|---|
| **What it produces** | Complete about page copy for a DTC brand: compelling headline, founding story, mission and values, founder bio, team mention, process/craft section, social impact section, and a closing CTA that drives visitors back to the product |
| **Turnaround** | 20-35 minutes per about page |
| **Quality bar** | Copy that makes a visitor feel something real — not a corporate mission statement, not a generic founder story, but a genuine human narrative that builds trust and affinity. The reader should finish the page liking the brand more than when they started. Total page copy should stay under 800 words. |
| **Best for** | New brand launches, about page rewrites, brand refreshes, Shopify store builds, and any time a brand's current about page reads like it was written by a committee or a chatbot |

## The Prompt

Paste the following prompt into Claude after filling in the brand variables above.

---

**PROMPT START**

You are a senior brand storyteller and copywriter who specializes in DTC about pages. Your about pages do not read like corporate bios or Wikipedia entries. They read like the best version of a conversation with the founder — honest, specific, warm, and genuinely interesting. Your goal is to make the visitor like and trust the brand more by the time they finish reading.

**Brand context:**

- Brand name: [BRAND_NAME]
- Brand voice: [BRAND_VOICE]
- Target audience: [TARGET_AUDIENCE]
- Products/services: [PRODUCT_SERVICE]
- Price range: [PRICE_RANGE]
- Key differentiator: [KEY_DIFFERENTIATOR]
- Competitors: [COMPETITOR_NAMES]
- Words to use: [WORDS_TO_USE]
- Words to avoid: [WORDS_TO_AVOID]
- Tone scale (1-10): Casual [__] | Playful [__] | Authoritative [__] | Luxurious [__] | Urgent [__]
- Campaign goal: [CAMPAIGN_GOAL]
- Additional context (founding story, founder info, team, process, impact): [ADDITIONAL_CONTEXT]

**Your assignment:**

Write complete about page copy for [BRAND_NAME]. The entire page must stay under 800 words total (all sections combined). Deliver every section described below in order. The page should read as one continuous, flowing piece — not a collection of disconnected blocks. A visitor should be able to scroll from top to bottom and feel like they just had a meaningful conversation with the brand.

**Section 1 — Headline**

Do NOT write "About Us" or "Our Story" or "Who We Are." These are wasted opportunities. Instead, write a headline (5-15 words) that captures the brand's reason for existing in a way that makes the visitor want to keep reading. The headline should hint at the story below — a statement, a belief, a question, or a provocation that sets the tone. It should feel like the opening line of a great essay, not a section header.

Good examples of the format (do not copy these — write one specific to this brand): "We ruined our kitchen before we built this company." "Good food should not require a PhD to find." "This started with a problem nobody was solving."

**Section 2 — Opening Story (150-200 words)**

Tell the brand's founding story, but start at the most interesting moment — NOT at the beginning. Do not open with "In 2019, [FOUNDER] decided to..." Instead, open with the specific frustration, realization, accident, or moment that made this brand inevitable. Drop the reader into a scene.

Requirements:
- Begin with a specific, concrete moment. A place, a time, a sensory detail, or a feeling. Make the reader see it.
- Introduce the founder by first name within the first two sentences. Make them a real person — not a LinkedIn bio.
- Cover the "before" (what was wrong), the "turning point" (what changed), and the "after" (what they built) — in that order.
- Make the founder relatable. Show vulnerability, frustration, obsession, or humor — something that makes the reader think "I get this person."
- Do not use the phrase "saw a gap in the market." Nobody has ever felt emotionally connected to a gap in a market.
- Do not make the founding story sound like a pitch deck. This is a human story, not an investor summary.
- Stay within 150-200 words. Tight storytelling is more powerful than long storytelling.

**Section 3 — Mission and Values (3-5 values)**

Write 3-5 brand values that are specific, concrete, and honest. Each value should include:

- A value name (2-5 words) — not generic single words like "Quality" or "Innovation." Instead, something with a point of view: "Ingredients You Can Google," "Slow on Purpose," "No Gatekeeping," "Built for Tuesday, Not Just Saturday."
- A description (1-2 sentences, 15-30 words) that explains what this value looks like in practice. Not what the brand believes — what the brand DOES because of this belief.

Rules for writing values:
- Do not use these overused value words: "passion," "innovation," "excellence," "integrity," "quality," "commitment." These are the default settings of every corporation on earth. They mean nothing.
- Every value must be specific enough that a competitor could NOT claim it without lying. "We care about quality" could be anyone. "Every batch is taste-tested by our team before it ships" is specific.
- At least one value should be slightly surprising or counterintuitive — something that makes the reader pause and think.
- Values should feel like real decisions the brand made, not inspirational poster quotes.

**Section 4 — The People (Founder bio 100 words + team mention)**

Write a founder bio of approximately 100 words. This is not a resume. It should:
- Be written in third person but feel warm, not formal.
- Include 1-2 personal details that make the founder human (a hobby, a quirk, a non-work passion, where they are based).
- Mention their relevant background or expertise in a natural way — woven into the narrative, not listed as credentials.
- End with something present-tense and forward-looking — what they are focused on now.

Follow the founder bio with a brief team mention (2-3 sentences). Acknowledge the team without making it feel like a corporate org chart. Mention the team's size, spirit, or a shared characteristic. If the team is small, lean into that as a strength.

**Section 5 — The Process / Craft (100-150 words)**

Write a section about how the product is made, sourced, or created. This section should:
- Make the reader appreciate the care and thought behind the product.
- Include at least one specific, concrete detail about the process that a mass-market competitor could not claim (e.g., "each bar is hand-tempered at 88 degrees" or "we source from three family farms in Oaxaca").
- Use sensory language where appropriate — the reader should almost be able to see, touch, or taste the process.
- Avoid vague claims like "we use only the finest ingredients" or "we go above and beyond." Show, do not tell.
- Connect the process to the customer benefit. The reader should understand why this process matters to THEM, not just to the brand.

**Section 6 — Social Impact (if applicable)**

If the brand has a social impact initiative, charitable partnership, sustainability practice, or community involvement, write a brief section (2-4 sentences, 40-60 words) about it. Rules:
- Be specific about what the brand does, not vague about what it "believes in."
- Include a real detail: a partner organization name, a specific percentage, a tangible outcome.
- Do not make this sound self-congratulatory. Frame it as something the brand does because it is part of who they are, not something they do for marketing points.
- If the client has not provided social impact information, include a brief note suggesting they add this section and offer examples of what it could include. Do not fabricate impact claims.

**Section 7 — Closing CTA**

End the about page with a CTA that transitions the reader from the brand story back to the product. This should:
- Feel like a natural ending to a conversation, not an abrupt sales pitch.
- Acknowledge what the reader just learned about the brand and pivot to the product as the natural next step.
- Include a headline or transition sentence (10-20 words) and a CTA button label (2-5 words).
- Suggest a specific link destination — the bestseller, the hero product, or a "start here" collection.
- The tone should be confident and inviting, as if the brand is saying: "Now that you know who we are, you are going to love what we make."

**Writing rules that apply to ALL sections:**
1. The entire about page must stay under 800 words total. Every word must earn its place. If a sentence does not make the reader like, trust, or understand the brand more, cut it.
2. Tell a real story. Real means specific. Real means showing, not telling. Real means moments, not summaries. If you find yourself writing "We are passionate about..." stop and rewrite with a concrete example instead.
3. Make the founder relatable. The reader should see themselves in the founder or feel like they would want to have a conversation with them. Vulnerability and specificity create connection. Polished perfection does not.
4. Write so the reader FEELS something. The about page should create an emotional response — warmth, admiration, surprise, humor, solidarity, or longing. If the copy is informative but emotionally flat, rewrite it.
5. Avoid these cliches and phrases: "saw a gap in the market," "passionate about," "committed to excellence," "on a mission to," "believe that everyone deserves," "it all started when," "from day one," "we're not just a [product] company." These are the language of brands with nothing real to say.
6. Never use "elevate," "game-changer," "revolutionize," "disrupt," "unlock," or "take your X to the next level."
7. Write in a mix of second and third person. The founding story will naturally be in third person (about the founder). The values and CTA should speak directly to the reader in second person ("you"). The transitions between perspectives should feel seamless.
8. Keep paragraphs short — 2-3 sentences maximum. The page should feel easy to read on a phone screen.

**PROMPT END**

## Quality Checks Before Sending

Run through every item on this list before delivering to the client. If any check fails, revise before sending.

1. **Word count compliance.** Count the total words across all sections. The entire about page must stay under 800 words. If it exceeds 800, identify the weakest sentences and cut them. Do not compress — remove. Tight copy is better than squeezed copy.

2. **The "feel something" test.** Read the entire page from top to bottom. Did you feel anything — warmth, admiration, surprise, humor, a sense of connection? If the page is informative but emotionally flat, identify the sections that feel generic and rewrite them with more specific, human details.

3. **Cliche scan.** Search the entire output for: "passionate about," "committed to," "on a mission to," "believe that everyone deserves," "saw a gap in the market," "it all started when," "from day one," "we're not just a [product] company," "game-changer," "elevate," "revolutionize," "disrupt," "unlock," "simply." Remove and replace every instance with specific, concrete language.

4. **Founder relatability check.** Read the founder bio and opening story. Does the founder feel like a real person you would want to talk to, or do they feel like a corporate headshot with a caption? Is there at least one personal detail, moment of vulnerability, or specific anecdote? If the founder feels generic, add specificity.

5. **Values specificity test.** Read each value name and description. Could a competitor copy any of these values word-for-word and have them still be true? If yes, that value is too generic. Rewrite it with a detail or practice that is unique to this brand.

6. **CTA naturalness check.** Read the closing CTA section. Does it feel like a natural transition from the brand story, or does it feel like an abrupt shift to "buy our stuff"? The CTA should feel like the organic next step in the conversation the page just had with the reader.

7. **Brand voice consistency.** Read the full page out loud. Does the tone stay consistent from the headline through the closing CTA? Does it match the tone scale ratings? Pay special attention to the transitions between third-person storytelling (the founder story) and second-person direct address (the values and CTA). These shifts should feel seamless.

## Example Output

The following example uses the fictional brand **Basecamp Nutrition** — a DTC sports nutrition company making clean, simple protein bars and recovery supplements for everyday athletes. Target audience: active adults 25-45 who work out 3-5 times a week but are not bodybuilders or professional athletes — they are runners, CrossFitters, weekend hikers, and gym regulars who want effective nutrition without the bro-science culture. Brand voice is straightforward, knowledgeable, and quietly confident — like a trainer who gives you the real answer, not the trending one. Price range: $28-$52. Key differentiator: formulated for real training, not for supplement store hype — every ingredient is dosed at clinically studied levels with no proprietary blends. Competitors: RXBAR, Momentous, Gainful. Founder: Jake Mercer, former collegiate track athlete turned food scientist.

---

# Basecamp Nutrition — About Page Copy

**Total word count:** 742 / 800 max

---

## HEADLINE

We got tired of supplements that looked better on the label than they worked in the gym.

---

## OPENING STORY

Jake Mercer was standing in the supplement aisle of a GNC in Austin, Texas, reading the back of a protein bar for the third time. The front of the package said "clean fuel for athletes." The ingredient list told a different story — maltodextrin, soy protein isolate, sucralose, and a proprietary blend that could have contained sawdust for all he knew. He was 26, two years out of collegiate track at Oregon, and training for his first marathon. He had a degree in food science. He knew what his body needed. And he could not find it on a single shelf.

That night, Jake started a spreadsheet. One column for what the research actually said about recovery nutrition. Another column for what supplement brands were actually putting in their products. The gap between those two columns was enormous — and infuriating. So he started making his own bars. First in his apartment kitchen, using a food processor and way too many sheet pans. Then in a shared commercial kitchen in East Austin, running batches after his day job. Friends on his running team tried them and stopped buying anything else.

That spreadsheet became Basecamp Nutrition. Three years later, every product in the line still starts the same way: with the research first, the formula second, and the marketing last.

---

## MISSION & VALUES

**Every Dose Does Something**
No filler ingredients, no fairy-dusting, no underdosed vitamins for label appeal. Every ingredient in every product is included at its clinically studied effective dose — and we publish the amounts right on the label.

**Built for Your Actual Tuesday**
We formulate for the person who trained hard this morning and still has a full day of work ahead. Basecamp products are designed for how real people actually train and recover, not for a photoshoot.

**No Proprietary Blends, Ever**
A proprietary blend is a brand telling you to trust them without showing their work. We show our work. Full transparency on every label because you deserve to know exactly what you are putting in your body.

**Boring Ingredients, Interesting Results**
We do not chase trending superfoods or exotic extracts. We use the well-studied, well-understood ingredients that actually perform — whey protein isolate, creatine monohydrate, tart cherry extract, magnesium glycinate. The unsexy stuff that works.

---

## THE PEOPLE

**Jake Mercer, Founder**
Jake is a food scientist, former 800m runner at the University of Oregon, and the person who personally reviews the certificate of analysis for every raw ingredient Basecamp sources. Before starting the company, he spent three years in product development at a major food manufacturer, where he learned exactly how brands cut corners on formulation without consumers ever knowing. He lives in Austin with his wife, their daughter, and a deeply unmotivated basset hound named Gus. When he is not reviewing lab reports, he is training for his next half marathon or arguing about coffee roast levels.

**The Team**
Basecamp is a team of nine, split between Austin and Portland. The group includes two food scientists, a logistics lead who has run supply chain for three supplement brands, and a customer experience team that actually uses the products they support. Nobody here came from a marketing-first supplement company. Everyone here trains.

---

## THE PROCESS

Every Basecamp product starts in the research, not in a brainstorm meeting. Jake and the formulation team review published clinical studies to identify the ingredients and doses that have demonstrated real results for recovery, endurance, and daily performance. That research becomes the formula. The formula gets prototyped in small test batches using ingredients sourced from audited suppliers — each one verified with a third-party certificate of analysis before it enters production.

The bars are made in a certified facility in Austin. The protein blend is mixed cold to protect amino acid integrity, and each batch is tested for heavy metals, microbial contaminants, and label accuracy by an independent lab. You will find the batch test results linked on every product page.

It takes longer and costs more to make products this way. That is a trade-off we accepted on day one because the alternative — selling you something with a great label and a mediocre formula — is exactly the problem we set out to fix.

---

## SOCIAL IMPACT

Basecamp donates 1% of annual revenue to the Travis Manion Foundation, which supports veterans through fitness-based community programs. We also supply product quarterly to three Austin-area running clubs that offer free training programs for underserved communities. This is not a campaign. It is a line item in our budget.
