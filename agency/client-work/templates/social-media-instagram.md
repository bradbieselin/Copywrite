### Weekly Content Calendar Structure

Follow this exact daily content mix:

- **Monday** — Educational / Tip post (teach something genuinely useful)
- **Tuesday** — Product Spotlight (show off product without sounding like an ad)
- **Wednesday** — Behind the Scenes / Founder Story (humanize the brand)
- **Thursday** — Customer Story / UGC-style (social proof that feels real)
- **Friday** — Fun / Entertaining (personality-forward, shareable content)
- **Saturday** — Lifestyle / Aspirational (paint the life the audience wants)
- **Sunday** — Community Question / Conversation starter (drive comments)

### Writing Rules You Must Follow

1. **Sound like a person, not a brand.** Write the way a smart, interesting friend would talk about this product. No corporate speak. No "we're thrilled to announce." No generic motivational quotes. Every caption should feel like it was written by a specific human being, not a marketing department.

2. **The hook must work without the image.** The first line of every caption should stop the scroll on its own — even if someone cannot see the photo. Use pattern interrupts, surprising statements, specific numbers, hot takes, or relatable observations. Never start with "Happy Monday!" or "Did you know?" or any generic opener.

3. **Vary the tone across the week.** Monday's educational post should sound different from Friday's fun post. Wednesday's founder story should have a different energy than Tuesday's product spotlight. The brand voice should be consistent but the emotional register should shift throughout the week.

4. **For carousel posts, provide complete slide-by-slide breakdowns.** When specifying a carousel, write out every single slide — the headline, the body text, and the visual direction for each slide. Carousels should have 5-10 slides, with the first slide being the hook and the last slide being a CTA or summary.

5. **For Reel scripts, keep them under 30 seconds.** Write the actual spoken script with timing cues. Include B-roll or visual direction notes. The script should be conversational and sound natural when spoken aloud — not like someone reading copy.

6. **Hashtag strategy must be layered.** For each post, provide 15-20 hashtags organized into three tiers: 5-7 broad/high-volume hashtags (500K+ posts), 5-7 mid-range niche hashtags (50K-500K posts), and 3-6 micro/branded hashtags (under 50K posts). Every set should be unique across the week.

7. **Engagement prompts must be specific.** Never end with "What do you think?" or "Drop a comment below!" Instead, give people a specific, easy-to-answer prompt that creates genuine conversation. Use "this or that" questions, fill-in-the-blank, hot take agreements, or ranking challenges.

8. **Visual direction must be actionable.** Do not just say "lifestyle photo." Describe the shot: lighting, composition, props, mood, color palette, text overlay placement, and any specific details that would help a photographer or designer execute the vision.

9. **Include strategic product mentions.** Not every post should sell. Product mentions should appear naturally in 3-4 posts per week. The other posts should build brand affinity and community without any product push.

10. **Format captions for mobile readability.** Use line breaks generously. No giant walls of text. Short paragraphs (1-3 sentences max). Use spacing to create rhythm and emphasis.

## Output Format

Each day should include all specified elements. The complete deliverable should contain:

- 7 complete posts (one per day, Monday through Sunday)
- Each post includes: Post type, best posting time, visual direction, hook, full caption (50-150 words), 15-20 hashtags in three tiers, engagement prompt
- Carousel posts include full slide-by-slide breakdowns (5-10 slides each)
- Reel posts include full scripts with timing cues (under 30 seconds) and text overlay timestamps
- At least 2 carousels and 1 reel in the weekly mix
- Captions formatted with line breaks for mobile readability

---

## Quality Checks Before Sending

Run through every single check before delivering to the client:

1. **Hook test:** Read only the first line of each caption in isolation. Does it stop the scroll? Would you pause if you saw it in your feed? If any hook sounds generic, rewrite it.

2. **Voice consistency test:** Read all 7 captions back to back. Do they sound like the same person wrote them? Does the voice match the brand brief? Flag any caption that sounds too corporate, too generic, or off-brand.

3. **Sell-to-value ratio:** Count how many posts directly mention or push the product. It should be no more than 4 out of 7. At least 3 posts should provide value, entertainment, or community without any sales angle.

4. **Hashtag uniqueness:** Confirm that no two posts share more than 3 hashtags. Each set should be substantially different. Verify hashtags are real and relevant (no made-up tags that would have zero posts).

5. **Caption length check:** Every caption must be between 50 and 150 words. Count the words. No exceptions. Trim or expand as needed.

6. **Engagement prompt quality:** Review each engagement prompt. Remove any that are generic ("What do you think?"). Every prompt must be specific enough that you can imagine real people answering it.

7. **Words-to-avoid scan:** Search every caption for any word or phrase from the client's "Words to Avoid" list. Remove and replace any that appear.

### SATURDAY — Lifestyle / Aspirational

**Post Type:** Feed Post (single image)

**Best Time to Post:** 9:00 AM EST — Saturday morning catches people planning their weekend adventures. Aspirational content inspires action when people have free time ahead.

**Visual Direction:**
Cinematic landscape photography. A lone hiker on a ridge at golden hour, mountains stretching behind them, warm amber light spilling across the scene. The hiker's backpack is open on a rock nearby with a Wild Bites bar casually placed on top — visible but not the focus. The image should feel like a National Geographic photo that happens to have a snack bar in it. Widescreen crop (4:5 for feed but with a panoramic composition feel). Colors: warm golds, deep greens, slate blues. The feeling should be "I need to be there right now."

**Hook (First Line):**
There's a version of your weekend that starts at a trailhead instead of a couch.

**Caption:**
There's a version of your weekend that starts at a trailhead instead of a couch.

Not the epic 14er summit version. Not the ultramarathon version. Just the version where you lace up, drive 30 minutes, and walk into the trees for a few hours.

That's the version most people skip. And it's the one that actually changes your week.

Pack water. Pack a bar. Leave your notifications in the car.

The trail doesn't need you to be impressive. It just needs you to show up.

See you out there.

### SUNDAY — Community Question

**Post Type:** Feed Post (single image with text overlay)

**Best Time to Post:** 10:00 AM EST — Sunday morning is peak engagement for conversation-starter posts. People are relaxed, browsing slowly, and more likely to comment thoughtfully.

**Visual Direction:**
Simple, bold text post on a natural background. The background should be a slightly blurred, warm-toned nature photograph (out-of-focus forest canopy, mountain texture, or rock face). Overlaid text should be large, bold, sans-serif font in white or cream. The question should be the visual — no product, no logo (maybe a small Wild Bites wordmark in the bottom corner, very subtle). The post should look more like a conversation card than an advertisement. Clean, minimal, inviting.

**Hook (First Line):**
Settle a debate that's been raging in the Wild Bites kitchen all week.

**Caption:**
Settle a debate that's been raging in the Wild Bites kitchen all week.

Jake says the best outdoor meal is a sunrise breakfast cooked on a camp stove after sleeping in a tent.

Maria says it's a lunch bar eaten at the summit with your legs dangling off the edge and nothing but sky in front of you.

They've been arguing about this for three days. We need outside opinions.

Best meal you've ever eaten outdoors. Where were you, what did you eat, and why was it perfect?

Go.
