### Daily Content Mix (5 tweets per day)

Each day must include exactly:
- **3 Standalone tweets** (under 280 characters each) — a mix of observations, opinions, tips, or personality
- **1 Quote-tweet-ready opinion** (under 200 characters) — a hot take or opinion that works as a response to industry news or competitor posts
- **1 Engagement tweet** (question, poll prompt, or conversation starter)

### Weekly Content Mix

Across the full week, the 35 tweets should include this approximate mix:
- **Product mentions:** 2-3 times per week, subtle and casual — never a hard sell. The product should come up naturally in context, not as the point of the tweet.
- **Industry commentary:** 2-3 times per week — opinions on trends, news, competitor moves, or industry problems. These should be sharp and position the brand/founder as a credible voice.
- **Founder personality:** 2-3 times per week — tweets that show the person behind the brand. Humor, personal observations, daily life moments that humanize the account.
- **Customer wins:** 1-2 times per week — sharing or referencing customer results, feedback, or stories. Must feel organic, not like a testimonial ad.
- **Engagement questions:** Daily (built into the 5th tweet each day) — questions, polls, "this or that" prompts, or hot take invitations that drive replies.

### Plus One Thread Per Week

One thread (8-12 tweets) on a strategic topic related to the brand's expertise or industry. The thread should:
- Deliver genuine, actionable value
- Establish the founder/brand as a knowledgeable voice
- Have a strong hook tweet that works as a standalone
- Include a natural product mention in the last 1-2 tweets (not forced)
- Be formatted with line breaks and numbered points for readability
- End with a CTA (follow, check out the product, share the thread)

### Writing Rules You Must Follow

1. **Write punchy, opinionated tweets.** Twitter rewards strong takes. Avoid wishy-washy, "both sides" language. Have a point of view. Every tweet should make someone want to reply, retweet, or argue.

2. **Use line breaks strategically.** Short lines hit harder on Twitter. Use white space. Break up thoughts. One idea per line. Let the reader's eye move quickly down the tweet.

3. **Keep product mentions casual.** Never write a tweet that reads like an ad. Product mentions should be woven into opinions, personal stories, or useful tips. "Been testing our new creatine for 3 weeks and the loading phase results are wild" beats "Introducing our new Creatine HCL — shop now!"

4. **Make the thread genuinely valuable.** Someone should be able to read the thread, never buy the product, and still feel like they gained something. The thread should be bookmarkable. Value first, product mention last.

5. **Match brand voice but edgier for Twitter.** Twitter audiences expect more edge, more personality, more willingness to be provocative. Take the brand voice and turn up the boldness by about 20%. The brand should sound slightly more unfiltered on Twitter than on Instagram.

6. **Every tweet under 280 characters.** No exceptions. Count characters carefully. Quote-tweet-ready opinions must be under 200 characters to leave room for the quoted tweet.

7. **No hashtags in tweets.** Hashtags look amateur on Twitter/X. The only exception is if there's a brand-specific hashtag or a major trending hashtag that's genuinely relevant.

8. **Vary the energy.** Not every tweet should be a hot take. Mix in genuine questions, quiet observations, humor, and vulnerability. An account that's always yelling is exhausting. An account that has range is compelling.

9. **Tweets should work in isolation.** Every tweet must make sense without context. Someone seeing a single tweet on their timeline should understand it, find it interesting, and want to follow the account.

10. **Reference the real world.** The best tweets feel timely and connected to the real world — cultural moments, shared experiences, common frustrations, seasonal relevance. Avoid generic "motivational" content that could be posted any time by any brand.

## Quality Checks Before Sending

Run through every single check before delivering to the client:

1. **Character count:** Verify every standalone tweet is under 280 characters and every quote-tweet-ready opinion is under 200 characters. Count carefully — Twitter counts characters strictly. Use a character counter tool if needed.

2. **Hot take test:** Read each opinion tweet in isolation. Does it provoke a reaction? Would someone feel compelled to reply or retweet? If a tweet is bland or could be posted by any brand, rewrite it with a sharper point of view.

3. **Sell ratio check:** Count all product mentions across the 35 tweets. There should be no more than 3 direct product references. If there are more, remove the least natural ones. Product mentions should never be the primary point of a tweet.

4. **Voice consistency test:** Read all 35 tweets back to back. Do they sound like the same person? Is the voice slightly edgier than the brand's Instagram voice? Flag any tweet that sounds too polished, too corporate, or too generic for Twitter.

5. **Thread value test:** Read the thread ignoring the product mention at the end. Is it still valuable? Would someone bookmark it even if they had no interest in the product? If the thread is just a long ad, rewrite it to lead with genuine expertise.

6. **Words-to-avoid scan:** Search every tweet and the full thread for any word or phrase from the client's "Words to Avoid" list. Remove and replace any that appear.

7. **Isolation test:** Pick any 5 tweets at random. Read each one completely out of context. Does each one make sense on its own? Is each one interesting enough that someone would follow the account based on that single tweet? If any tweet needs context to work, rewrite it.
