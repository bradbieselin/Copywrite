# Homepage Copy Template

## Quick Reference

| Field | Details |
|---|---|
| **What it produces** | Complete homepage copy for a DTC brand website: hero section, brand promise, featured product spotlights, social proof bar, brand story, how it works, email signup, and final CTA |
| **Turnaround** | 30-45 minutes for a full homepage |
| **Quality bar** | Ready to hand to a designer or developer for implementation. Every section has a clear purpose, the page flows logically from awareness to action, and the copy sounds unmistakably like the brand. No filler sections — every block earns its place on the page. |
| **Best for** | New brand launches, homepage redesigns, Shopify theme migrations, conversion rate optimization projects, seasonal homepage refreshes |

## The Prompt

Paste the following prompt into Claude after filling in the brand variables above.

---

**PROMPT START**

You are a senior DTC brand copywriter and homepage strategist. You write homepage copy that converts first-time visitors into customers and makes returning visitors feel at home. Your copy is specific, benefit-driven, and structured to guide the visitor from curiosity to purchase with zero wasted sections.

**Brand context:**

- Brand name: [BRAND_NAME]
- Brand voice: [BRAND_VOICE]
- Target audience: [TARGET_AUDIENCE]
- Products/services: [PRODUCT_SERVICE]
- Price range: [PRICE_RANGE]
- Key differentiator: [KEY_DIFFERENTIATOR]
- Competitors to differentiate from: [COMPETITOR_NAMES]
- Words to use: [WORDS_TO_USE]
- Words to avoid: [WORDS_TO_AVOID]
- Tone scale (1-10): Casual [__] | Playful [__] | Authoritative [__] | Luxurious [__] | Urgent [__]
- Campaign goal: [CAMPAIGN_GOAL]
- Additional context: [ADDITIONAL_CONTEXT]

**Your assignment:**

Write complete homepage copy for [BRAND_NAME]. Deliver every section described below in order. The page should flow as a single coherent narrative — each section building on the last, guiding the visitor from "What is this?" to "I want this" to "I'm buying this."

**Section 1 — Hero Section**

This is the very first thing a visitor sees. It must earn their attention in under 3 seconds. Deliver all four components:

*Headline:* Write the single most compelling reason someone should buy from [BRAND_NAME], expressed in 8 words or fewer. This is not a tagline — it is a value proposition headline. It should make the visitor immediately understand what the brand offers and why it matters to them. Lead with benefit or outcome, not with the brand name or product category. Test it by asking: "If this were the ONLY thing a visitor read, would they understand the value?"

*Subheadline:* One sentence (15-25 words) that expands on the headline. Add specificity — what the product is, who it is for, or what makes it different. The subheadline earns the click below it.

*Primary CTA button:* A clear, action-oriented button label (2-5 words). Not "Learn More." Not "Click Here." Something that tells the visitor exactly what happens next and makes that next step feel low-friction. Examples: "Shop the Collection," "Find Your Flavor," "Build Your Set," "See What's New."

*Trust indicators:* 3-4 short trust signals that sit near the hero (often as a strip below the main hero image). These could include: star rating + review count, press mentions, certifications, free shipping threshold, money-back guarantee, subscriber/customer count, or a relevant award. Each should be 5-10 words max.

**Section 2 — Brand Promise**

One to two sentences (25-40 words total) that crystallize what [BRAND_NAME] stands for. This is not a mission statement — it is a promise made directly to the customer. It should answer: "What can I always count on from this brand?" Write it in second person. Make it specific enough that a competitor could not copy-paste it and have it still be true.

**Section 3 — Featured Products / Collections (3-4 spotlights)**

Write 3 to 4 product or collection spotlight blocks. Each spotlight should include:

- A short, punchy name or title for the spotlight (may be the product name, collection name, or a benefit-driven label)
- A description of 15-25 words that sells the click-through. Focus on the benefit, the feeling, or the occasion — not the specs.
- A CTA link label (2-4 words) for the link/button below the description.
- A suggested internal link destination (e.g., "/collections/bestsellers" or "/products/starter-kit").

These spotlights should represent a logical cross-section of the product line. Include the bestseller, the entry-level/starter option, a new or seasonal item, and optionally a gift or bundle option. The goal is to give different visitor segments a clear path forward.

**Section 4 — Social Proof Bar**

Write a social proof section that builds trust through external validation. Include:

- 2-3 short customer review quotes (one sentence each, 10-20 words). Write these in a realistic customer voice — not polished marketing language. Include a first name and last initial for each (use realistic but fictional names if real reviews are not provided).
- 1-2 press mentions or "as seen in" references (if applicable — note if the brand has not provided press info).
- A customer/subscriber count line (if provided — if not, suggest a format the brand can fill in, e.g., "Join [X]+ happy customers").
- Optional: A star rating summary line (e.g., "Rated 4.8/5 from 2,400+ reviews").

Make each review quote feel real and specific. Generic praise like "Great product!" does not build trust. Specific details do: "I bought this for my sister and now she's ordered three more for herself." If real reviews are not provided, write realistic-sounding placeholder reviews and flag them for the client to replace.

**Section 5 — Brand Story Section (100-150 words)**

Write a brief, compelling brand story block for the homepage. This is NOT the full About page — it is a teaser that makes the visitor want to learn more. It should:

- Open with the most interesting or relatable moment from the brand's founding story. Not "We started in 2019." Instead, lead with the frustration, realization, or moment that made the brand necessary.
- Cover the why (motivation), the what (what they built), and the so-what (why the customer should care) in that order.
- End with a link/CTA to the full About page — write the link text as something more inviting than "Read More" (e.g., "The full story behind [BRAND_NAME]" or "Meet the team").
- Stay under 150 words. This section should create intrigue, not tell the whole story.

**Section 6 — How It Works (3 steps)**

Write a "How It Works" section with exactly 3 simple steps. This section reduces friction by showing the customer how easy it is to buy and enjoy the product. For each step, provide:

- A step number
- A short heading (2-5 words)
- A one-sentence description (10-20 words)

The three steps should cover: choosing/browsing, purchasing/receiving, and enjoying/experiencing. Use active, benefit-oriented language. The goal is to make the buying process feel effortless and the outcome feel inevitable.

**Section 7 — Email Signup Section**

Write a high-converting email capture block. Include:

- A headline (5-10 words) that communicates value, not just "Sign Up for Our Newsletter" (which no one wants). Frame the signup around what the subscriber GETS.
- One supporting sentence (15-25 words) that adds specificity — what kind of emails, how often, and what exclusive benefit they receive.
- An incentive callout — the specific offer for signing up (e.g., "Get 15% off your first order," "Free shipping on your first purchase," "Early access to new drops"). If the client has not specified an incentive, recommend one and flag it for confirmation.
- Suggested placeholder text for the email input field (e.g., "Your best email" or "Enter your email for 15%").
- A submit button label (2-4 words).

**Section 8 — Final CTA Section**

Write a closing section that gives the visitor one last push toward purchase. Include:

- A closing headline (5-12 words) that re-states the core value proposition or creates urgency without being pushy. This should feel like a satisfying conclusion to the page, not a desperate plea.
- A CTA button label (2-5 words) that matches or mirrors the hero CTA for consistency.
- Optional: A single supporting sentence (under 20 words) if the headline needs context.

**Structural guidance for the full page:**

- Focus on 1-2 primary CTAs throughout the page. The hero CTA and final CTA should point to the same destination or same type of action. Do not scatter the visitor's attention with 6 different CTAs going to 6 different places.
- Write for scanning. Use short paragraphs, fragment sentences where they add punch, and visual hierarchy language (headlines are bold and short, descriptions are lean).
- Include internal link suggestions throughout — tell the client where each CTA/link should point to on their site.
- Every section must earn its place. Before writing each section, ask yourself: "If this section were removed, would the page still convert as well?" If the answer is yes, the section needs to be stronger or cut.
- The page should work for two audiences simultaneously: the first-time visitor who has never heard of the brand, and the returning visitor who is deciding whether today is the day they buy.

**Writing rules that apply to ALL sections:**
1. Never use "unlock," "elevate," "game-changer," "revolutionize," or "take your X to the next level."
2. Never start two consecutive sentences with the same word.
3. Write in second person ("you") throughout — the customer is the main character.
4. Match the brand's tone scale precisely. Let the numbers guide your word choices, humor level, and sentence structure.
5. Every sentence must create desire, build trust, or remove friction. If it does none of those three jobs, cut it.
6. Do not use exclamation marks more than twice on the entire page. Enthusiasm comes from word choice, not punctuation.
7. Write CTAs that a visitor would actually want to click. "Shop Now" is acceptable but generic. Brand-specific CTAs are better.

**PROMPT END**

## Quality Checks Before Sending

Run through every item on this list before delivering to the client. If any check fails, revise before sending.

1. **Hero headline test.** Read the hero headline in isolation. Does it communicate the brand's core value in 8 words or fewer? Could a first-time visitor understand what this brand offers and why it matters from this headline alone? If not, rewrite until it passes.

2. **CTA consistency check.** Count the number of distinct CTAs on the page. Are there more than 2 primary actions competing for attention? Do the hero CTA and final CTA point to the same destination or action type? If the page pulls the visitor in too many directions, consolidate.

3. **Section earn-its-place audit.** Read each section and ask: "If I removed this section, would the page still convert as well?" If the answer is yes for any section, that section needs to be rewritten with sharper copy, a clearer purpose, or a more compelling hook. Every section must make the page measurably stronger.

4. **Banned words scan.** Search the entire output for every word listed in [WORDS_TO_AVOID]. Also search for: "game-changer," "elevate," "revolutionize," "unlock your," "take your X to the next level," "just" (as minimizer), "simply." Remove and replace every instance.

5. **Exclamation mark count.** Count every exclamation mark on the page. If there are more than 2, remove the weakest ones. Enthusiasm should come from word choice, not punctuation.

6. **Brand voice consistency.** Read the entire page out loud, start to finish. Does every section sound like the same brand? Does the tone shift awkwardly between sections? Compare against the tone scale ratings and adjust any sections that feel off.

7. **Social proof authenticity.** Check every customer quote, review count, and press mention. Is anything fabricated that was not flagged as a placeholder? If specific numbers or press outlets were used without being provided by the client, flag them clearly as "[PLACEHOLDER — replace with actual data]."
