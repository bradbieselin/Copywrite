Hey [FIRST_NAME],

Grab a supplement from your cabinet. Any one. Got it? Good.

Here's the 3-minute label test:

**Check 1: Is it a proprietary blend?**
Look for "Proprietary Blend," "Performance Matrix," "Energy Complex," or any branded compound name followed by a total weight with multiple ingredients listed. If yes — you have no idea how much of each ingredient you're getting. Red flag.

**Check 2: Are the key ingredients at clinical doses?**
Google "[ingredient name] clinical dose" for each active ingredient. Compare to what's on the label. If the label says 1g but the research says 3g — you're getting a sprinkle, not a dose.

**Check 3: Is it third-party tested?**
Look for NSF Certified for Sport, Informed Sport, or USP verification. If none of these? The brand is self-reporting purity. That's like grading your own homework.

**Preview text:** The "anabolic window" is mostly a myth. Here's what actually matters.

Hey [FIRST_NAME],

The fitness industry has been telling you to chug a protein shake within 30 minutes of your workout or your gains will evaporate. Let's look at what the research actually says.

**The "anabolic window" is mostly a myth.**

A 2013 meta-analysis in the Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition found that total daily protein intake matters far more than timing. The "30-minute window" is real, but it's more like a 4–6 hour window, and it only matters significantly if you trained fasted.

**What actually matters for recovery:**

1. **Total daily protein:** 1.6–2.2g per kg of bodyweight. This is the single biggest factor.
2. **Distribution:** Spread protein across 3–5 meals/snacks. Your body can only synthesize ~25–40g per meal effectively.
3. **Pre-workout nutrition:** If you ate within 3 hours of training, post-workout timing is less critical.
4. **Sleep:** This is where the real recovery happens. Protein before bed (specifically casein) has strong research support.
