## Brand Voice Summary

GlowRoot sounds like your smartest friend who also happens to be a cosmetic chemist — someone who knows exactly which ingredients actually work and isn't afraid to call out the ones that don't. The brand talks TO its customers like an insider sharing the real story behind skincare, not AT them like a glossy magazine ad. While competitors in the clean beauty space lean into vague "natural" buzzwords and aesthetic Instagram grids, GlowRoot leads with ingredient transparency and measurable results. There's a warmth to the voice — it's confident without being clinical, knowledgeable without being pretentious, and it never takes itself so seriously that it forgets skincare should feel good, not stressful.

## Voice Attributes

### 1. Transparent
- **What it means:** GlowRoot names ingredients, explains why they're there, and tells you what they actually do at the concentration used. No hiding behind proprietary blends or vague "botanical extracts."
- **DO:** "Our Radiance Serum uses 15% vitamin C (L-ascorbic acid at a pH of 3.5) — the form dermatologists actually recommend, at a concentration that works."
- **DON'T:** "Powered by our exclusive GlowComplex blend of natural ingredients."

### 2. Confident
- **What it means:** GlowRoot states what their products do without hedging. They don't use "may help" or "could potentially." If they can't make a direct claim, they cite the research.
- **DO:** "This serum fades dark spots. Period. 89% of users saw visible improvement in 4 weeks."
- **DON'T:** "This serum may help with the appearance of uneven skin tone over time."

### 3. Warm
- **What it means:** The brand feels like a knowledgeable friend, not a dermatologist's office. Copy is approachable, uses "you" and "your skin," and acknowledges that skincare is personal.
- **DO:** "Your skin has been through a lot. Let's give it something that actually works."
- **DON'T:** "Clinical-grade formulation for optimal dermal rejuvenation."

### 4. No-BS
- **What it means:** GlowRoot calls out industry nonsense — overpriced products, meaningless certifications, ingredients that are there for the label and not for your skin.
- **DO:** "That $120 moisturizer? Same active ingredients as ours, plus a fancy jar and a celebrity endorsement. We skipped both."
- **DON'T:** Generic clean beauty language that sounds like every other brand on the shelf.
