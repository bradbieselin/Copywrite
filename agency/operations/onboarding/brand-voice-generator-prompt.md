# Brand Voice Bible Generator Prompt

## Purpose

This is the master prompt used to transform a completed CopyDTC Client Questionnaire into a comprehensive Brand Voice Bible. The Bible becomes the single source of truth for all content created for the client. Every writer, every piece of content, every channel references this document.

## How to Use

1. Copy the full prompt below.
2. Paste it into a new Claude conversation.
3. Attach or paste the client's completed questionnaire immediately after the prompt.
4. Review the output for accuracy, tone, and completeness.
5. Make manual edits based on your own expertise and any additional context from the kickoff call.
6. Format as a PDF and deliver to the client within 48 hours of receiving the questionnaire.

---

## THE PROMPT

---

You are a world-class brand strategist and DTC copywriter. You have built brand voice systems for hundreds of direct-to-consumer brands generating between $500K and $50M+ in annual revenue. You understand that a brand's voice is not a vague feeling -- it is a precise, documented system that allows any skilled writer to produce on-brand content without guessing.

Your task is to analyze the completed client questionnaire below and produce a Brand Voice Bible. This document will be used by copywriters to produce all content for this brand -- emails, ads, landing pages, social media, product descriptions, SMS, and more. It must be specific enough that two different writers reading it would produce content that sounds like the same brand.

Do not write generic advice. Do not produce filler. Every sentence in this Bible should be actionable and specific to THIS brand based on the questionnaire answers. If the questionnaire is vague on a point, use your expertise to make a smart, specific recommendation based on the brand's industry, audience, price point, and overall positioning -- but flag it as a recommendation rather than a client-stated fact.

Produce the Brand Voice Bible with the following sections. The total output should be 2,000-3,000 words. Write in a direct, clear, professional tone. Use headers, bullet points, and tables for scannability. This document will be used daily by working copywriters -- make it practical, not theoretical.

---

### SECTION 1: Brand Voice Summary

Write 3-4 vivid, specific sentences that capture the overall voice of this brand. This is the "elevator pitch" of the brand voice -- if a writer read nothing else, these sentences should give them 80% of what they need to know. Do not use vague language like "friendly and professional." Be precise and evocative.

Use this structure:
- Sentence 1: The overall personality and energy of the brand.
- Sentence 2: How it relates to the customer -- the dynamic between brand and buyer.
- Sentence 3: What makes the voice distinctive compared to competitors in the space.
- Sentence 4 (optional): A surprising element or tension in the voice that keeps it interesting.

Example of a strong Brand Voice Summary (do NOT copy this -- create one unique to the client):
"[Brand] sounds like your most knowledgeable friend who also happens to be funny as hell. We talk TO our customers, never AT them -- like we're sharing insider tips over coffee, not lecturing from a podium. While competitors in the supplement space hide behind lab coats and clinical jargon, we translate the science into language that makes people say 'oh, THAT'S why I feel like garbage.' We're confident without being cocky, nerdy without being boring, and we never take ourselves so seriously that we forget we're selling vitamins, not curing cancer."

---

### SECTION 2: Voice Attributes

Identify 4-5 core voice attributes for this brand. These are the defining characteristics that should be present in every piece of content.

For each attribute, provide:

**Attribute Name** (one word or short phrase)

- **What it means for this brand:** 2-3 sentences explaining what this attribute looks like in practice for THIS specific brand. Not a dictionary definition -- a practical guide.
- **DO (This):** 3 specific examples of copy that demonstrate this attribute well. Write actual sample copy snippets, not descriptions.
- **DON'T (Not That):** 3 specific examples of copy that a writer might produce if they misunderstand this attribute. Write actual sample copy snippets showing what to avoid.

Choose attributes based on the client's questionnaire answers -- particularly their 5 brand words (Q18), their anti-words (Q19), their casual/formal scale (Q20), humor scale (Q21), and the brands they admire (Q27) and dislike (Q28). The attributes should capture the distinctive tensions in the brand voice -- the things that make it specific, not the things that could apply to any brand.

---

### SECTION 3: Tone Spectrum

The brand voice stays consistent, but the TONE shifts depending on the channel. A brand might be playful on Instagram and more authoritative in email. The voice is WHO the brand is. The tone is HOW the brand adjusts to different contexts.

Create a table or structured list covering these 8 channels. For each channel, specify:

- **Channel name**
- **Tone adjustment:** 1-2 sentences on how the tone shifts for this channel.
- **Energy level:** Low / Medium / High
- **Formality shift:** More casual / Neutral / More formal (relative to baseline)
- **Example line:** One sentence of sample copy in the correct tone for that channel.

Channels to cover:
1. **Website** (homepage, product pages, about page)
2. **Email Marketing** (campaigns, newsletters, promotional sends)
3. **Email Flows** (welcome series, abandoned cart, post-purchase, winback)
4. **Instagram** (captions, stories, Reels text)
5. **TikTok** (video scripts, captions, comments)
6. **Twitter/X** (posts, replies, threads)
7. **Paid Ads** (Meta ads, Google ads, TikTok ads)
8. **SMS** (promotional texts, flow texts)

Base these adjustments on the client's customer demographics (Q11, Q16), their brand's casual/formal scale (Q20), humor level (Q21), and where their audience spends time (Q16).

---

### SECTION 4: Language Rules

This section is the hard-and-fast rulebook. No ambiguity. Writers should be able to check their copy against these rules like a checklist.

Organize into these sub-sections:

**Always Use:**
List specific words and phrases this brand should use regularly. Pull from the client's questionnaire answers (Q25), their product language, their customer's language (Q12, Q14, Q17), and any branded terms. Include 10-15 words/phrases minimum.

**Never Use:**
List specific words and phrases this brand must avoid. Pull from Q26, and add any words that conflict with the brand's positioning, voice attributes, or customer expectations. Include 10-15 words/phrases minimum with a brief reason for each.

**Grammar & Style Rules:**
List 8-12 specific rules covering:
- Contractions (based on Q22)
- Slang usage (based on Q23)
- Cursing policy (based on Q24)
- Sentence length preferences
- Paragraph length preferences
- Oxford comma (yes/no)
- Capitalization style (sentence case, title case, all caps for emphasis, etc.)
- Exclamation point usage (how often, in what contexts)
- Emoji usage (yes/no, which ones, how often)
- Number formatting (spell out or use digits)
- How to handle product names (capitalization, trademark symbols, etc.)

**Power Words:**
List 15-20 high-impact words that align with the brand voice and resonate with the target customer. These are words that should appear frequently across all content. Organize them by emotional category (urgency, trust, desire, exclusivity, etc.).

**Signature Phrases:**
Create 5-8 brand-specific phrases or sentence structures that writers can use as building blocks. These aren't taglines -- they're recurring linguistic patterns that make the brand sound like itself. For example, a brand might always start email CTAs with "Ready to..." or use "here's the thing:" as a transition.

---

### SECTION 5: Customer Language Map

Great copy uses the customer's own words. This section maps the language the target customer uses so writers can mirror it back in content.

Create these sub-sections based on Q12, Q13, Q14, Q15, and Q17:

**How They Describe Their Problem:**
List 8-12 phrases and sentences the target customer would actually say or type when describing the problem the product solves. These should sound like real human language -- the way someone would text a friend, post in a Facebook group, or search on Google. Include both emotional and practical language.

**How They Describe Their Desired Outcome:**
List 8-12 phrases and sentences the customer would use to describe what they want their life/situation to look like AFTER using the product. Focus on outcomes and feelings, not product features.

**Their Objections (and How to Handle Them):**
For each of the top 3 objections from Q15, provide:
- The objection in the customer's exact words
- The emotional root of the objection (what are they really worried about?)
- 2-3 copy angles that address this objection effectively
- A sample sentence that handles the objection naturally in content

**Their Purchase Triggers:**
List 5-8 specific situations, emotions, or events that cause someone to go from "browsing" to "buying." These are the moments we want to tap into in email subjects, ad hooks, and CTAs. For each trigger, include a sample hook or headline that leverages it.

---

### SECTION 6: Competitive Voice Positioning

Based on the client's answers about competitors (Q13, Q14) and the brand's positioning, create a brief competitive voice analysis.

**How Competitors Sound:**
For each of the 2-4 main competitors (identify these from context clues in the questionnaire), write 1-2 sentences describing their voice and tone. What do they lean on? What do they all sound like?

**The Gap:**
In 2-3 sentences, describe the voice gap in the market -- what NO competitor is doing that this brand can own. This is the brand's voice differentiation.

**Positioning Statement:**
Write one powerful sentence that captures how this brand's voice stands apart. Format: "While [competitors] sound like [X], [brand] sounds like [Y]."

---

### SECTION 7: Content Principles

List 5 non-negotiable content principles for this brand. These are the strategic rules that sit above individual copy decisions. They guide the WHAT and WHY of content, not just the HOW.

Each principle should be:
- A short, memorable statement (5-10 words)
- Followed by 2-3 sentences explaining what it means in practice
- Followed by one specific example of how it applies to real content

These principles should be unique to this brand and derived from their goals (Q37-41), their customer (Q11-17), their positioning (Q14), and their brand personality (Q18-29).

---

### SECTION 8: Sample Copy

Produce 8 sample copy pieces that demonstrate the Brand Voice Bible in action. Each piece should feel like real, publish-ready content for this brand. Writers will use these as reference points for tone, style, and structure.

Write the following 8 samples:

1. **Homepage Headline + Subheadline:** A compelling homepage hero section (headline + 1-2 sentence subheadline + CTA button text).

2. **Instagram Caption:** A full Instagram caption (hook line + body + CTA + relevant hashtags) for a product feature post. 150-250 words.

3. **Email Subject Line Collection:** 10 email subject lines spanning different types: promotional, educational, seasonal, urgency, curiosity, and personal. Include preview text for each.

4. **Welcome Email Opening:** The first 150-200 words of a welcome email (Email 1 of a welcome flow). Set the tone for the entire customer relationship.

5. **Facebook/Meta Ad - Primary Text:** A full Meta ad (primary text + headline + description) for the brand's best-selling product. Use a proven ad framework (PAS, AIDA, or story-based) appropriate for the brand.

6. **Product Description:** A complete product description (250-350 words) for the brand's top-selling product. Include headline, body copy, and bullet points.

7. **Customer Support Response:** A response to a customer who received a damaged product. Show how the brand voice comes through even in customer service -- maintaining personality while being genuinely helpful and making it right.

8. **SMS Promotional Message:** A 160-character (or appropriately brief) SMS promoting a limited-time offer. Include urgency without sounding spammy.

For each sample, include a brief note (1 sentence, in italics) explaining which voice attributes and language rules are demonstrated.

---

### SECTION 9: Voice Don'ts

List 5-7 specific red flags that indicate content has gone off-brand. These are the common mistakes a writer might make if they're not careful. Frame these as concrete, recognizable patterns -- not vague warnings.

For each Voice Don't, provide:
- **The Don't:** A clear, specific statement of what to avoid.
- **Why It's Off-Brand:** 1 sentence explaining why this violates the brand voice.
- **What It Looks Like:** An example of off-brand copy that commits this error.
- **What to Do Instead:** A rewritten version that fixes the problem.

Base these on the client's anti-words (Q19), the copy they hate (Q28), their industry norms to avoid, and any patterns that would undermine their brand positioning.

---

### SECTION 10: Quick Reference Card

Create a one-page summary that a writer can keep open while working. This is the "cheat sheet" version of the full Bible. It should fit on a single page and include:

- **Voice in 1 Sentence:** The Brand Voice Summary condensed to one line.
- **5 Voice Attributes:** Listed with one-phrase descriptions.
- **Tone Scale:** A visual or shorthand representation (casual-formal number, humor number).
- **Top 5 Always-Use Words/Phrases**
- **Top 5 Never-Use Words/Phrases**
- **Key Grammar Rules:** (contractions, cursing, emoji, exclamation points -- 4-5 critical rules)
- **Customer's Core Problem in Their Words:** 1 sentence.
- **Customer's Desired Outcome in Their Words:** 1 sentence.
- **Top 3 Objections:** Listed as short phrases.
- **Brand Is / Brand Is NOT:** A quick 5-item comparison. Format: "We ARE [x]. We are NOT [y]."

---

### FORMATTING INSTRUCTIONS

- Use markdown headers (##, ###) for sections.
- Use bold for emphasis and labels.
- Use bullet points and numbered lists for scannability.
- Use tables where they improve readability.
- Do not use filler phrases like "In conclusion" or "As mentioned above."
- Write in second person ("your brand," "you") when addressing the brand, and third person ("the customer," "they") when describing the audience.
- Keep individual sentences under 25 words where possible.
- Every single line should be useful. If it's not actionable, cut it.

---

### FINAL CHECK

Before delivering, verify:
- [ ] Every section is specific to THIS brand, not generic advice.
- [ ] Sample copy sounds distinctly different from competitors.
- [ ] Voice attributes capture real tension (not just "friendly and professional").
- [ ] Language rules are concrete enough to enforce in a copy review.
- [ ] Customer language sounds like real humans, not marketing personas.
- [ ] The Quick Reference Card works as a standalone cheat sheet.
- [ ] Total output is between 2,000 and 3,000 words.
- [ ] No filler. No fluff. No "In today's competitive marketplace..."
