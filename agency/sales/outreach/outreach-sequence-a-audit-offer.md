# Outreach Sequence A: The Audit Offer

> **Purpose:** 3-email cold outreach sequence for prospects whose content clearly needs improvement. The hook: you've spotted a specific weakness and prepared a free audit showing exactly what's broken and how to fix it.
> **Used by:** Brad for cold outreach to DTC brands with visible content gaps
> **Sequence length:** 3 emails over 8 days
> **Target:** DTC brands doing $500K–$10M with existing content programs that have clear room for improvement

---

## Sequence Overview

| Email | Day | Goal | Versions |
|-------|-----|------|----------|
| Email 1: The Opening | Day 1 | Get them to reply "yes, send it" | 3 versions (A/B/C test) |
| Email 2: The Follow-Up | Day 4 | Add value, reignite conversation | 2 versions (A/B test) |
| Email 3: The Final Touch | Day 8 | Soft close — last clear offer | 1 version |

---

## Rules for All Emails

- Under 100 words per email (75 is better)
- No formal greetings ("Hi [FIRST_NAME]," is fine. "Dear [FIRST_NAME]," is not.)
- No "I hope this email finds you well" or any variation
- No "just following up" or "just checking in" or "just wanted to"
- No "pick your brain" or "hop on a quick call" in the first 2 emails
- No links in Email 1 (triggers spam filters)
- Lowercase subject lines (feels personal, not corporate)
- Every email readable in under 15 seconds
- Tone: you're a peer who noticed something and wants to help, not a salesperson who wants their money
- Placeholders: [FIRST_NAME], [COMPANY], [SPECIFIC_OBSERVATION], [CONTENT_WEAKNESS], [THEIR_CATEGORY], [SIMILAR_RESULT]

## Email 1: The Opening

**Send timing:** Day 1
**Goal:** Get them to reply "yes, send it"

---

### Version A: The Direct Approach

**Subject line options (pick one):**
1. quick note about [COMPANY]'s content
2. spotted something on [COMPANY]'s site
3. [COMPANY]'s [CONTENT_WEAKNESS] — quick thought
4. one thing I'd fix on [COMPANY]'s site
5. [FIRST_NAME], noticed this about [COMPANY]

**Body:**

> Hi [FIRST_NAME],
>
> I was looking at [COMPANY] and [SPECIFIC_OBSERVATION]. Really solid brand — but your [CONTENT_WEAKNESS] is working against you.
>
> I put together a quick audit breaking down what's off and how to fix it. We build content systems for DTC brands doing $500K–$10M, so this is our wheelhouse.
>
> Want me to send it over? No strings attached.
>
> Brad

**Personalization points:** [SPECIFIC_OBSERVATION] and [CONTENT_WEAKNESS] must reference something real you found during research.

**Internal notes:** This version works best for prospects where the content gap is obvious and easy to articulate in one sentence. Don't soften the observation — be direct but not rude.

---

### Version B: The Value-First Approach

**Subject line options (pick one):**
1. most [THEIR_CATEGORY] brands miss this
2. the [CONTENT_WEAKNESS] problem in [THEIR_CATEGORY]
3. why [THEIR_CATEGORY] emails underperform
4. what top [THEIR_CATEGORY] brands do differently
5. [THEIR_CATEGORY] content doesn't have to be this hard

**Body:**

> Hi [FIRST_NAME],
>
> Most DTC [THEIR_CATEGORY] brands are leaving money on the table with their [CONTENT_WEAKNESS]. [COMPANY] has a great product — but from what I can see, your content isn't doing it justice.
>
> We build content systems for DTC brands doing $500K–$10M, and this is a pattern we fix all the time. I put together a free audit for [COMPANY] showing exactly where the gaps are and what to do about them.
>
> If you're curious, I'll send it over this week.
>
> Brad

**Personalization points:** [THEIR_CATEGORY] should be specific (e.g., "skincare," "pet food," "home fitness") not generic. [CONTENT_WEAKNESS] should name the channel or asset type (e.g., "welcome emails," "product page copy," "ad creative").

**Internal notes:** This version works best when you can tie the prospect's problem to a broader industry pattern. Leads with insight, not the ask.

---

### Version C: The Mutual Connection / Shared Context Approach

**Subject line options (pick one):**
1. fellow [PLATFORM] nerd checking in
2. [COMPANY] caught my eye on [PLATFORM]
3. working with [THEIR_CATEGORY] brands on [PLATFORM]
4. one [THEIR_CATEGORY] brand to another
5. been looking at [COMPANY] on [PLATFORM]

**Body:**

> Hi [FIRST_NAME],
>
> I work with a lot of brands on [PLATFORM] and was checking out [COMPANY] — your [GENUINE_COMPLIMENT] really stands out. But your [CONTENT_WEAKNESS] isn't keeping up with the rest.
>
> I put together a quick audit showing what I'd change and why. Happy to send it over if it's useful.
>
> Brad

**Personalization points:** [PLATFORM] = Shopify, Klaviyo, Meta, etc. [GENUINE_COMPLIMENT] must be real and specific (e.g., "product photography," "brand voice on Instagram," "subscription model"). [CONTENT_WEAKNESS] should feel like a natural contrast to the compliment.

**Internal notes:** This version works best when you share a clear context — same platform, same industry, same tools. The compliment-then-gap structure makes the observation feel balanced, not critical.

## Email 2: The Follow-Up

**Send timing:** Day 4
**Goal:** Add value and reignite the conversation

---

### Version A: The Quick Win

**Subject line:** Reply to the original thread — no new subject. Start with "Re: [original subject]"

**Body:**

> Hi [FIRST_NAME],
>
> Quick thought for [COMPANY] — your homepage CTA says something generic like "Shop Now," but a specific CTA like "Find Your [PRODUCT_TYPE]" typically converts 15–25% better for DTC brands. Small change, big lift.
>
> I put together a full audit with more like this. Want it?
>
> Brad

**Personalization points:** Replace the example tip with something real you found during research. The tip MUST be specific to their site and actionable today. Generic advice kills this email.

**Internal notes:** The goal is to prove you actually looked at their stuff. One genuinely useful tip does more selling than any pitch. Keep the tip concrete — a specific CTA, subject line, or flow they should change.

---

### Version B: The Social Proof Nudge

**Subject line:** Reply to the original thread — no new subject. Start with "Re: [original subject]"

**Body:**

> Hi [FIRST_NAME],
>
> We recently helped a DTC [SIMILAR_CATEGORY] brand increase their email revenue by [SIMILAR_RESULT] by rebuilding their flows. Their setup looked a lot like [COMPANY]'s does right now.
>
> I still have that audit ready for you — want me to send it over?
>
> Brad

**Personalization points:** [SIMILAR_CATEGORY] should be close to their category but doesn't need to be exact. [SIMILAR_RESULT] should be a real number — don't fabricate results. If you don't have a case study yet, anonymize a real engagement (e.g., "a DTC supplement brand" instead of the client name).

**Internal notes:** This version works best when you have a relevant result to reference. If you don't have a close match, use Version A instead. Never fake a case study.

---

## Email 3: The Final Touch

**Send timing:** Day 8
**Goal:** Last chance. Brief, direct, soft close.

**Subject line:** Reply to the original thread — no new subject. Start with "Re: [original subject]"

**Body:**

> Hi [FIRST_NAME],
>
> Figured I'd check in one more time — we're taking on 2 more brands this month, and I think [COMPANY] would be a strong fit based on what I've seen.
>
> If the timing's not right, no worries at all. If it is, reply and I'll send the audit right over.
>
> Brad

**Internal notes:** This is a breakup email done right. No guilt. No "I know you're busy." No passive aggression. Just a clear, final offer with a soft time element. If they don't reply, move them to a nurture list and revisit in 60–90 days.

## Personalization Research Checklist

**Time budget: 5 minutes per prospect. Do this before sending Email 1.**

- [ ] **Visit their website.** Read the homepage hero and 2 product pages. Note: is the copy generic or sharp? Are CTAs specific or default "Shop Now" buttons?
- [ ] **Sign up for their email list** (use a test email). Note: do they have a welcome sequence? How many emails? Does it feel branded or templated?
- [ ] **Check their Instagram/TikTok.** Note: posting frequency, engagement rate, quality of captions. Are they creating content or just reposting product shots?
- [ ] **Check their Meta Ad Library** (facebook.com/ads/library). Note: are they running ads? How many? What does the creative look like? Is the copy strong or generic?
- [ ] **Write down the ONE biggest content gap in 10 words or less.** This becomes your [SPECIFIC_OBSERVATION]. If you can't articulate it in one sentence, keep looking.

**Examples of strong observations:**
- "Your welcome flow is 1 email — most top brands run 4–6"
- "Your ad copy leads with features, not outcomes"
- "Your product pages have no social proof below the fold"
- "Your email subject lines are all 'New Arrivals' variants"

**Examples of weak observations (don't use these):**
- "Your content could be better" (too vague)
- "Your branding is off" (not actionable)
- "You should do more email" (not specific)

---

## Disqualification Criteria

**When NOT to send this sequence:**

- **Too small:** Company has fewer than 10 products (probably not generating enough revenue to invest in content)
- **Not investing yet:** No active social media presence (they're not spending on marketing — you'd be selling the concept, not the execution)
- **Too early stage:** Website looks like it was built yesterday (no established content to audit — nothing to anchor your observation to)
- **Already has an agency:** They're already working with a known agency (check their email footer, LinkedIn team page, or "built by" credits on site)
- **Content is actually good:** Their content is sharp, flows are dialed in, ads are strong — don't force a problem that isn't there. Send them Sequence C ("The Results Lead") instead.

## Success Metrics

- Target open rate: 45%+
- Target reply rate: 8–12%
- Target audit-to-proposal rate: 50%+
- Track per-version performance to identify winning variants after 30+ sends each
