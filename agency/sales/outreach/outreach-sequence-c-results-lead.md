# Outreach Sequence C: The Results Lead

## Purpose
3-email outreach sequence that leads with proven results. Instead of pointing out what's broken, you show what's possible. The hook: you've achieved specific, measurable results for a similar brand and you're confident you can do the same for them.

**Used by:** Brad for warm-ish outreach to DTC brands that already have decent content but want better performance.

**Sequence length:** 3 emails over 10 days

**Target:** DTC brands doing $500K–$10M that are already investing in content but not getting the results they should be. These are brands with existing email flows, landing pages, and ad creative — they just aren't optimized.

---

## When to Use This Sequence

This is your best sequence when:
- The prospect's content is OK but not great (Sequence A's "broken content" angle would feel like a stretch)
- You have a relevant case study or result from a similar brand or category
- The prospect is likely already working with someone (freelancer, in-house, or agency) and you need to show why switching is worth it
- The prospect's brand is strong enough that leading with criticism would be off-putting

Do NOT use this sequence if:
- You don't have a real result to reference (never fabricate numbers)
- The prospect's content is genuinely terrible (use Sequence A instead — they need to hear what's broken)
- You haven't worked with a brand in their category or an adjacent one (the results won't feel relevant)

---

## Sequence Overview

| Email | Day | Goal | Versions |
|-------|-----|------|----------|
| Email 1: The Result | Day 1 | Lead with a specific result and offer to show how | 3 versions (A/B/C test) |
| Email 2: The Method | Day 5 | Explain the approach behind the result | 2 versions (A/B test) |
| Email 3: The Offer | Day 10 | Clear, specific offer with a soft deadline | 1 version |

---

## Rules for All Emails

- Under 100 words per email (75 is better)
- No formal greetings ("Hi [FIRST_NAME]," is fine. "Dear [FIRST_NAME]," is not.)
- No "I hope this email finds you well" or any variation
- No "just following up" or "just checking in"
- Every result you reference MUST be real. Anonymize if needed, but never fabricate.
- Lowercase subject lines (feels personal, not corporate)
- Every email readable in under 15 seconds
- Tone: confident peer sharing what worked, not a braggy salesperson
- No links in Email 1 (triggers spam filters)

**Placeholders:** `[FIRST_NAME]`, `[COMPANY]`, `[THEIR_CATEGORY]`, `[SIMILAR_BRAND]`, `[RESULT_METRIC]`, `[RESULT_NUMBER]`, `[CONTENT_TYPE]`, `[TIMEFRAME]`

---

## Case Study Matching Guide

Before using this sequence, match the prospect to your most relevant result. Use this matrix:

| Prospect Profile | Best Case Study Match | Why It Works |
|-----------------|----------------------|-------------|
| DTC skincare/beauty | Another beauty brand's email results | Same audience psychology, similar buying patterns |
| DTC supplements/health | Any health/wellness brand's content results | Regulatory-aware, education-heavy content needs |
| DTC food/beverage | Another food brand's conversion results | Impulse purchase optimization, subscription angle |
| DTC fashion/apparel | Any lifestyle brand's ad or social results | Visual-first, trend-driven content |
| DTC home/lifestyle | Any DTC brand's email or landing page results | Broad applicability of email and CRO tactics |

**If you don't have an exact match:** Use an adjacent category. "A DTC wellness brand" is close enough for a supplements prospect. "A DTC lifestyle brand" works for home goods. The result just needs to feel plausible for their business.

**If you don't have ANY relevant result yet:** Don't use this sequence. Use Sequence A (Audit Offer) or Sequence B (Competitor Gap) instead. You can switch to Sequence C once you have your first 1-2 client results.

## Email 1: The Result

**Send timing:** Day 1

**Goal:** Lead with a specific, impressive result. Make them curious about how.

### Version A: The Specific Number

**Subject line options (pick one):**
- `how a [THEIR_CATEGORY] brand hit [RESULT_NUMBER]`
- `[RESULT_NUMBER] in [TIMEFRAME] for a [THEIR_CATEGORY] brand`
- `[THEIR_CATEGORY] + [CONTENT_TYPE] = [RESULT_NUMBER]`
- `what we did for a brand like [COMPANY]`

**Body:**

> Hi [FIRST_NAME],
>
> We recently helped a DTC [THEIR_CATEGORY] brand [RESULT_METRIC] by [RESULT_NUMBER] in [TIMEFRAME] — just by rebuilding their [CONTENT_TYPE].
>
> Their setup before the rebuild looked a lot like [COMPANY]'s does right now.
>
> I think we could do something similar for you. Want me to walk you through what we changed and why?
>
> Brad

**Personalization points:**
- `[RESULT_METRIC]` + `[RESULT_NUMBER]` must be real. Examples: "increase email revenue by 47%," "boost welcome flow conversion by 3.2x," "cut cost-per-acquisition by 35%."
- `[TIMEFRAME]` should be specific: "60 days," "the first 90 days," "in Q4."
- "Looked a lot like [COMPANY]'s does right now" only works if it's true. Check their email flows, landing pages, or ads to verify the comparison holds.

**Internal notes:** This is your highest-converting Email 1 variant when you have a killer result. The number does the heavy lifting — keep the rest short.

### Version B: The Before/After Story

**Subject line options (pick one):**
- `a [THEIR_CATEGORY] brand's content before and after`
- `what changed when we rebuilt a [THEIR_CATEGORY] brand's [CONTENT_TYPE]`
- `[THEIR_CATEGORY] brand story — thought of [COMPANY]`
- `from generic to [RESULT_NUMBER]`

**Body:**

> Hi [FIRST_NAME],
>
> Quick story: a DTC [THEIR_CATEGORY] brand came to us with [BEFORE_STATE]. Their [CONTENT_TYPE] was fine but not driving real results.
>
> We rebuilt it from scratch. [TIMEFRAME] later: [RESULT_NUMBER].
>
> Looking at [COMPANY], I see the same starting point. Happy to share exactly what we did and how it could apply to your brand.
>
> Brad

**Personalization points:**
- `[BEFORE_STATE]` should be relatable and specific: "a 2-email welcome flow and templated product descriptions," "generic Meta ads with declining ROAS," "a blog with no SEO strategy."
- The before/after arc makes the result feel achievable, not magical.

**Internal notes:** Use this version when the result alone isn't jaw-dropping but the transformation story is compelling. A 25% improvement sounds modest in isolation but impressive when you explain they went from "1 welcome email" to "a full 5-email revenue-driving flow."

### Version C: The "Brands Like Yours" Approach

**Subject line options (pick one):**
- `what [THEIR_CATEGORY] brands are doing with content right now`
- `[COMPANY]-sized brands are seeing [RESULT_NUMBER] from [CONTENT_TYPE]`
- `results we're seeing for brands like [COMPANY]`
- `[THEIR_CATEGORY] content is working — here's proof`

**Body:**

> Hi [FIRST_NAME],
>
> We work with a handful of DTC [THEIR_CATEGORY] brands around [COMPANY]'s size, and the results we're getting from [CONTENT_TYPE] keep surprising us.
>
> Latest win: [RESULT_NUMBER] for a brand that started in a similar spot to where [COMPANY] is now.
>
> If you're curious what that could look like for your brand, I'm happy to break it down.
>
> Brad

**Personalization points:**
- "Around [COMPANY]'s size" implies you know their revenue range. Only use this if you've verified they're in the $500K-$10M range (check SimilarWeb traffic estimates, employee count, or social following as proxies).
- This version works well when you have multiple results to draw from, even if none are a perfect category match.

**Internal notes:** This is the safest Version C — it positions you as someone who works with brands at their level without making any aggressive claims. Good for prospects who might be skeptical of cold outreach.

## Email 2: The Method

**Send timing:** Day 5

**Goal:** Pull back the curtain on how you got the result. Give them just enough to prove you know what you're doing, not so much that they try to DIY it.

### Version A: The Framework

**Subject line:** Reply to the original thread — no new subject. Start with "Re: [original subject]"

**Body:**

> Hi [FIRST_NAME],
>
> Following up — here's the short version of what we did for that [THEIR_CATEGORY] brand:
>
> 1. Built a Brand Voice Bible from a deep-dive questionnaire (so every piece of content sounds like them, not like AI)
> 2. Rebuilt their [CONTENT_TYPE] using conversion-tested templates
> 3. Delivered [DELIVERABLE_COUNT] pieces in the first 30 days
>
> The whole system runs on AI, which is how we move 10x faster than a traditional agency.
>
> Want me to put together a custom breakdown for [COMPANY]?
>
> Brad

**Personalization points:**
- `[DELIVERABLE_COUNT]` should be real. If your Starter tier delivers 20/month, say "20+ pieces."
- The 3-step framework should be honest about your process. Don't oversell.

**Internal notes:** This email works because it's transparent about your method without giving away the actual system. The "Brand Voice Bible" and "AI-powered" angle are your differentiators — lean into them.

### Version B: The Quick Insight

**Subject line:** Reply to the original thread — no new subject. Start with "Re: [original subject]"

**Body:**

> Hi [FIRST_NAME],
>
> One thing from the work we did for that [THEIR_CATEGORY] brand that might be useful for [COMPANY]:
>
> The biggest lever was [SPECIFIC_TACTIC]. Most brands skip this or do it halfway. When we got it right, [RESULT_METRIC] moved by [RESULT_NUMBER] in the first [TIMEFRAME].
>
> I think the same approach would work for [COMPANY]. Happy to show you what it'd look like.
>
> Brad

**Personalization points:**
- `[SPECIFIC_TACTIC]` should be one concrete thing: "a 5-email post-purchase sequence," "benefit-first product descriptions instead of feature lists," "segmented abandoned cart flows by cart value."
- This email works best when the tactic is something the prospect obviously isn't doing yet.

**Internal notes:** Version B is more targeted than Version A. Use it when you can identify one specific tactic that would clearly apply to the prospect's situation. If you're not sure which tactic to highlight, Version A's broader framework approach is safer.

## Email 3: The Offer

**Send timing:** Day 10

**Goal:** Clear, direct offer with a soft time element. No tricks, no pressure — just a specific next step.

**Subject line:** Reply to the original thread — no new subject. Start with "Re: [original subject]"

**Body:**

> Hi [FIRST_NAME],
>
> Last one from me.
>
> We've got room for [NUMBER] more brands this month. Based on what we did for [SIMILAR_BRAND] and what I've seen from [COMPANY], I think we could get you [REALISTIC_OUTCOME] within the first 60 days.
>
> If you're interested, I'll put together a free content audit for [COMPANY] — no strings attached. Takes us about 30 minutes and it's yours to keep either way.
>
> Worth a shot?
>
> Brad

**Personalization points:**
- `[NUMBER]` should be small and real (2-3). Creates natural scarcity without feeling like a pressure tactic.
- `[REALISTIC_OUTCOME]` — be conservative. "A fully automated email system generating revenue on autopilot" is better than "10x your revenue."
- The free audit offer bridges this sequence into the Sequence A workflow. If they say yes, generate the audit using `sales/audits/audit-generator-prompt.md`.

**Internal notes:** This is a soft close. You're not asking for a call or a contract — you're offering a free audit. That's the lowest-friction next step. If they say yes, the audit does the selling for you. If they don't reply, add them to a nurture list and revisit in 60-90 days.

## Personalization Research Checklist

**Time budget:** 5 minutes per prospect. Do this before sending Email 1.

1. **Verify they're the right size:** Check SimilarWeb for traffic estimates, LinkedIn for employee count, or social media following as revenue proxies. You want brands doing $500K-$10M.

2. **Check their content baseline:** Sign up for their email list. Visit 2-3 product pages. Look at their Meta Ad Library. You need to confirm their content is OK but not great (if it's terrible, use Sequence A instead).

3. **Match them to a case study:** Using the matching guide above, find your most relevant result. Write down the one-line version: "[SIMILAR_BRAND] went from [BEFORE] to [AFTER] in [TIMEFRAME]."

4. **Identify the one thing you'd change first:** Even though this sequence leads with results, you still need to know what you'd actually do for them. This becomes your `[CONTENT_TYPE]` and informs Email 2.

5. **Check for disqualifiers:** Already working with a known content agency? Content is already excellent? Too small or too early-stage? If yes to any, skip this prospect or use a different sequence.
