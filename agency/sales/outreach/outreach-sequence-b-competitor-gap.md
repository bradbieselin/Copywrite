# Outreach Sequence B: Competitor Gap Analysis

## Purpose
4-email outreach sequence that leads with competitive intelligence. You show the prospect how their competitors are out-messaging them, then offer to close the gap. This sequence works because nobody wants to lose to a competitor — especially on something fixable like content.

**Used by:** Brad for cold outreach to DTC brands in competitive niches where competitor comparison is compelling

**Sequence length:** 4 emails over 14 days

**Target:** DTC brands doing $500K–$10M in competitive categories (skincare, supplements, fashion, food, fitness, pet) where at least one direct competitor is clearly winning the content game.

---

## Sequence Overview

| Email | Day | Goal | Versions |
|-------|-----|------|----------|
| Email 1: The Comparison Hook | Day 1 | Spark curiosity with a competitor observation | 3 versions (A/B/C test) |
| Email 2: The Insight Drop | Day 4 | Deliver one specific competitive insight | 2 versions (A/B test) |
| Email 3: The Gap Closer | Day 8 | Show what "better" looks like for their brand | 1 version |
| Email 4: The Breakup + Gift | Day 14 | Final offer with a mini teardown attached | 1 version |

---

## Rules for All Emails

- Under 125 words per email (100 is better)
- No formal greetings ("Hi [FIRST_NAME]," is fine. "Dear [FIRST_NAME]," is not.)
- No "I hope this email finds you well" or any variation
- No "just following up" or "just checking in" or "just wanted to"
- No trashing the competitor — frame it as "they're doing X well" not "you're doing X badly"
- No links in Email 1 (triggers spam filters)
- Lowercase subject lines (feels personal, not corporate)
- Every email readable in under 20 seconds
- Tone: you're a strategist sharing an interesting observation, not a salesperson pitching
- **Never fabricate competitive data.** If you can't find a real gap, don't use this sequence for that prospect.

**Placeholders:** `[FIRST_NAME]`, `[COMPANY]`, `[COMPETITOR]`, `[THEIR_CATEGORY]`, `[COMPETITOR_STRENGTH]`, `[PROSPECT_GAP]`, `[SPECIFIC_DATA_POINT]`

## Email 1: The Comparison Hook

**Send timing:** Day 1

**Goal:** Spark curiosity by referencing a competitor who's doing something specific better.

### Version A: The Direct Comparison

**Subject line options (pick one):**
- `[COMPETITOR] is doing something [COMPANY] isn't`
- `noticed something between [COMPANY] and [COMPETITOR]`
- `[COMPANY] vs [COMPETITOR] — one thing stood out`
- `something [COMPETITOR] does that [COMPANY] should steal`

**Body:**

> Hi [FIRST_NAME],
>
> I was researching [THEIR_CATEGORY] brands and compared [COMPANY] side by side with [COMPETITOR].
>
> Your product is just as strong (maybe stronger), but [COMPETITOR]'s [COMPETITOR_STRENGTH] is pulling ahead — and it's driving real results for them.
>
> I put together a quick comparison showing exactly where [COMPANY] is losing ground and what it would take to flip the script.
>
> Want me to send it over?
>
> Brad

**Personalization points:**
- `[COMPETITOR]` must be a real, recognizable competitor in their space. Not a giant like Nike — pick someone in their weight class.
- `[COMPETITOR_STRENGTH]` should be specific: "email welcome sequence," "Meta ad copy," "product page storytelling" — not vague like "marketing."

**Internal notes:** This version works best when you can name a competitor the prospect would recognize and care about losing to. If the competitor isn't well-known in their niche, use Version B instead.

### Version B: The Category Insight

**Subject line options (pick one):**
- `the [THEIR_CATEGORY] brand winning on content right now`
- `who's winning [THEIR_CATEGORY] content (and why)`
- `[THEIR_CATEGORY] content — one brand is pulling away`
- `the content gap in [THEIR_CATEGORY]`

**Body:**

> Hi [FIRST_NAME],
>
> I've been studying content across the [THEIR_CATEGORY] space, and there's a clear split forming.
>
> A few brands are building real content engines — automated email flows, conversion-focused landing pages, platform-specific ad copy. The rest are posting and hoping.
>
> [COMPANY] has the product to compete with anyone. But from what I can see, your [PROSPECT_GAP] is putting you in the second group.
>
> I put together a short breakdown showing where you stand vs. the top performers. Happy to share it.
>
> Brad

**Personalization points:**
- `[PROSPECT_GAP]` should be the single most obvious content weakness: "welcome emails," "product descriptions," "paid ad creative."
- Keep the category framing broad enough to feel credible but narrow enough to feel relevant.

**Internal notes:** This version works when you don't have a single clear competitor to name but can speak to the category-level trend. Good for fragmented markets with lots of similar brands.

### Version C: The Data-Led Approach

**Subject line options (pick one):**
- `[SPECIFIC_DATA_POINT] — quick [THEIR_CATEGORY] finding`
- `a number that caught my eye about [COMPANY]`
- `[THEIR_CATEGORY] benchmark your team should know`
- `[COMPANY]'s [PROSPECT_GAP] vs the benchmark`

**Body:**

> Hi [FIRST_NAME],
>
> Quick data point: top-performing DTC [THEIR_CATEGORY] brands are seeing [SPECIFIC_DATA_POINT]. From what I can tell, [COMPANY] isn't there yet.
>
> Not a knock — most brands aren't. But the gap is fixable, and it usually comes down to [PROSPECT_GAP].
>
> I put together a comparison showing where [COMPANY] stacks up and what the top performers are doing differently.
>
> Worth a look?
>
> Brad

**Personalization points:**
- `[SPECIFIC_DATA_POINT]` must be real and verifiable. Examples: "40-50% open rates on welcome flows," "3-5x ROAS on UGC-style Meta ads," "$2+ revenue per email subscriber per month."
- Only use this version if you have access to actual benchmark data for their category.

**Internal notes:** This version works best for data-driven founders (ex-finance, ex-tech) who respond to numbers over narratives. If the prospect's LinkedIn shows a creative or brand-first background, use Version A or B instead.

## Email 2: The Insight Drop

**Send timing:** Day 4

**Goal:** Deliver one specific, valuable competitive insight that proves you've done real research.

### Version A: The Screenshot Play

**Subject line:** Reply to the original thread — no new subject. Start with "Re: [original subject]"

**Body:**

> Hi [FIRST_NAME],
>
> Quick follow-up — I dug deeper into the [COMPANY] vs. [COMPETITOR] comparison.
>
> One thing that jumped out: [COMPETITOR]'s [SPECIFIC_CONTENT_ASSET] does [SPECIFIC_THING_IT_DOES_WELL]. [COMPANY]'s version [SPECIFIC_THING_MISSING].
>
> That one difference is probably worth [ESTIMATED_IMPACT] in [REVENUE/CONVERSIONS/ENGAGEMENT].
>
> I've got a full breakdown ready. Say the word and I'll send it your way.
>
> Brad

**Personalization points:**
- `[SPECIFIC_CONTENT_ASSET]` = "welcome email sequence," "product page hero section," "Meta ad library," etc.
- `[SPECIFIC_THING_IT_DOES_WELL]` must describe an actual observable difference. Example: "leads with a customer testimonial and a specific discount code" vs. "just says Welcome to Our Store."
- `[ESTIMATED_IMPACT]` — be conservative. "A few percentage points on conversion" is more credible than "doubling your revenue."

**Internal notes:** This email lives or dies on the specificity of the insight. If your observation sounds like something you could say about any brand, rewrite it. The reader should think "wait, they actually looked at my stuff."

### Version B: The Industry Pattern

**Subject line:** Reply to the original thread — no new subject. Start with "Re: [original subject]"

**Body:**

> Hi [FIRST_NAME],
>
> Following up with something I keep seeing in [THEIR_CATEGORY]:
>
> The brands growing fastest right now have all invested in [CONTENT_PATTERN] — usually 3-4 [SPECIFIC_DELIVERABLE_TYPE] that run on autopilot and drive [METRIC] without extra ad spend.
>
> [COMPANY] has the brand and the product to make this work. Your [PROSPECT_GAP] is the bottleneck.
>
> I've got the full competitive comparison ready whenever you want it.
>
> Brad

**Personalization points:**
- `[CONTENT_PATTERN]` = "automated email flows," "conversion-optimized landing pages," "platform-native ad creative"
- `[SPECIFIC_DELIVERABLE_TYPE]` = "email sequences," "landing page variants," "ad copy sets"
- `[METRIC]` = "email revenue," "on-site conversion," "ROAS"

**Internal notes:** Use this version when you couldn't find a single killer competitive insight but can speak to a broader category pattern. Less sharp than Version A, but still useful when combined with a strong Email 1.

## Email 3: The Gap Closer

**Send timing:** Day 8

**Goal:** Show what "better" looks like — give them a taste of what their content could be.

**Subject line:** Reply to the original thread — no new subject. Start with "Re: [original subject]"

**Body:**

> Hi [FIRST_NAME],
>
> Last thing on this — I mocked up what [COMPANY]'s [PROSPECT_GAP] could look like if it matched what top [THEIR_CATEGORY] brands are doing.
>
> Here's the short version:
>
> **Now:** [ONE_SENTENCE_CURRENT_STATE]
> **Better:** [ONE_SENTENCE_IMPROVED_STATE]
>
> The full audit shows exactly how to get there — channel by channel, with specifics.
>
> Want me to send it over this week?
>
> Brad

**Personalization points:**
- The "Now vs. Better" comparison needs to be concrete and specific to their brand. Example:
  - **Now:** "Your welcome flow is 1 generic email with no discount."
  - **Better:** "A 4-email sequence that introduces your story, highlights your bestseller, drops a first-purchase incentive, and recovers non-buyers — automated and running 24/7."
- This email is your best conversion opportunity. Spend extra time making the "Better" description vivid and specific.

**Internal notes:** This is the highest-converting email in the sequence because you're showing, not telling. The "Now vs. Better" format makes the gap impossible to ignore. If this email doesn't convert them, Email 4 gives you one final shot.

## Email 4: The Breakup + Gift

**Send timing:** Day 14

**Goal:** Last contact. Attach a mini one-page teardown so they get value even if they don't reply.

**Subject line:** Reply to the original thread — no new subject. Start with "Re: [original subject]"

**Body:**

> Hi [FIRST_NAME],
>
> Last note from me on this.
>
> I went ahead and put together a one-page content teardown for [COMPANY] — top 3 things I'd change and why. It's attached.
>
> No pitch, no follow-up after this. If the timing works down the road, you know where to find me.
>
> Brad
>
> P.S. We're taking on 2-3 more [THEIR_CATEGORY] brands this quarter. If [COMPANY] ends up being one of them, I think we'd do great work together.

**Attachment:** A one-page PDF or Google Doc with:
1. **The biggest content gap** (1-2 sentences + what to fix)
2. **The quick win** (one change they can make today without hiring anyone)
3. **The long-term play** (what a proper content system would look like for their brand)

Use `sales/audits/audit-generator-prompt.md` to create a shortened version of a full audit. Keep it to one page. Include the CopyDTC logo and contact info at the bottom.

**Internal notes:** This is a breakup email, but with a gift. The one-page teardown does two things: (1) it gives them value even if they never reply, building long-term goodwill, and (2) it's something they might forward to a colleague or revisit later. Many "dead" prospects from this sequence come back 30-90 days later because they kept the teardown.

If they don't reply after Email 4, add them to a nurture list. Revisit in 60-90 days with a new angle (Sequence A or C).

## Competitor Research Guide

**Time budget:** 10 minutes per prospect. Do this before sending Email 1.

### Step 1: Identify 1-2 Direct Competitors
- **SimilarWeb:** Enter their domain → check "Competitors" tab for traffic-based competitors
- **Meta Ad Library** (facebook.com/ads/library): Search their category → find brands running similar ads
- **Google search:** "[their category] best brands" or "[their category] Shopify stores"
- **Shopify top stores:** Use BuiltWith or Store Leads to find similar-sized brands in their niche

### Step 2: Compare Content Side by Side
Pick 2-3 of these to compare:

| What to Compare | Where to Find It | What "Winning" Looks Like |
|----------------|------------------|--------------------------|
| Welcome email flow | Sign up for both brands' email lists | 4-6 emails over 7-10 days, branded, includes discount |
| Product page copy | Visit top 2 product pages each | Story-driven, benefit-focused, social proof below fold |
| Meta ad creative | Meta Ad Library | Multiple active variants, UGC-style, strong hooks |
| Homepage hero | Visit both sites | Clear value prop, specific CTA, trust signals visible |
| Email subject lines | Check inbox after signup | Personalized, curiosity-driven, A/B tested |

### Step 3: Find the Gap
Write down ONE sentence that captures the biggest competitive content gap. This becomes your `[PROSPECT_GAP]`.

**Strong gap statements:**
- "[COMPETITOR] runs a 6-email welcome flow with a loyalty program intro. [COMPANY] sends one generic welcome email."
- "[COMPETITOR]'s Meta ads use UGC and specific benefit claims. [COMPANY]'s ads are product-on-white with generic taglines."
- "[COMPETITOR]'s product pages tell a story with customer reviews inline. [COMPANY]'s read like a spec sheet."

**Weak gap statements (don't use):**
- "[COMPETITOR] has better marketing" (too vague)
- "[COMPETITOR] spends more on ads" (not a content gap)
- "[COMPANY] needs better branding" (not actionable)

### Step 4: Frame it Right
The goal is never to trash the prospect. The framing is always:
- "Your product is great. Your content isn't keeping up."
- "Your competitor has figured this one thing out. You can too."
- "This isn't about spending more. It's about messaging smarter."

---

## Disqualification Criteria

**When NOT to use this sequence:**

- **No clear competitor advantage:** If the prospect's content is roughly equal to competitors, Sequence A (Audit Offer) is a better fit.
- **Prospect is the category leader:** If they're already the top brand, competitive comparison falls flat. Use Sequence C (Results Lead) instead.
- **Tiny niche with no comparable brands:** If you can't find a real competitor to reference, don't force it.
- **Competitor relationship:** If the competitor you'd reference is also a prospect or client, skip it.
