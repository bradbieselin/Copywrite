# CopyDTC Pricing Guide

## Purpose
Internal reference for pricing conversations, proposals, and negotiations. This guide explains what each tier includes, why it's priced that way, and how to handle common pricing objections.

**Used by:** Brad for sales calls, proposals, and pricing questions from prospects.

**Last updated:** March 2026

---

## Tier Structure

### Starter — $3,000/month

**Who it's for:** Brands doing $500K–$2M in revenue. They know they need content but don't have the budget or volume for a full content engine. Usually early-stage DTC brands with 1-2 hero products.

**What's included:**
- Brand Voice Bible (built during onboarding, maintained ongoing)
- 20 deliverables per month
- Typical mix: 4-5 email sequences (welcome, abandoned cart, post-purchase), 4-5 product descriptions, 4-5 social posts, 2-3 ad copy sets
- 1 round of revisions per deliverable
- Bi-weekly performance check-in (async, via email or Loom)
- Content calendar for the month (delivered Day 1)

**What's NOT included:**
- Landing pages (add-on)
- Blog posts / SEO content (add-on)
- Strategy calls (upgrade to Growth)
- Unlimited revisions (upgrade to Scale)

**Delivery cadence:** All 20 deliverables delivered within the first 15 days. Revisions completed by Day 25. Client has final assets in hand before the end of the month.

**Your margin at this tier:** ~85-90%. Your time investment is roughly 8-10 hours per client per month once systems are dialed in. The Brand Voice Bible and templates do the heavy lifting.

**Why $3,000:** This is the sweet spot for early-stage DTC brands. Below $3K, you attract clients who aren't serious about content. Above $3K, they expect a full content engine (which is Growth tier). At $3K, you're cheaper than a full-time junior copywriter ($4-5K/month) but faster and more consistent.

### Growth — $5,000/month

**Who it's for:** Brands doing $2M–$7M in revenue. They're past the "we need content" stage and into "we need a content system." They probably have someone in-house doing content, but it's inconsistent or underperforming.

**What's included:**
- Everything in Starter, plus:
- 40 deliverables per month
- Typical mix: Full email system (5-6 flows), 4-6 landing pages, 4-6 blog posts (SEO-optimized), 8-10 ad copy sets (Meta + Google), 6-8 social posts, 4-6 product descriptions
- 2 rounds of revisions per deliverable
- Weekly strategy check-in (15-minute call or async Loom)
- Content performance reporting (monthly, showing engagement metrics and recommendations)
- Conversion optimization suggestions based on content performance data

**What's NOT included:**
- Dedicated strategist (upgrade to Scale)
- Unlimited revisions (upgrade to Scale)
- A/B testing management (add-on)

**Delivery cadence:** 10 deliverables per week, delivered on Monday. Revisions turned around within 48 hours. Weekly check-in on Thursdays.

**Your margin at this tier:** ~80-85%. Time investment is roughly 15-18 hours per client per month. The additional strategy component (performance reporting, optimization suggestions) takes more time but justifies the price jump.

**Why $5,000:** At this price, you replace a freelance copywriter ($3-4K) AND a content strategist ($2-3K) for less than either would cost alone. The value proposition is clear: better content, faster delivery, and actual strategy — for less than they'd pay for a mediocre full-time hire.

---

### Scale — $10,000/month

**Who it's for:** Brands doing $5M–$10M+ in revenue. They have a marketing team but need a content partner who can operate at their speed. They've probably worked with agencies before and been burned by slow turnarounds and generic copy.

**What's included:**
- Everything in Growth, plus:
- Unlimited deliverables (within reason — this is a partnership, not an all-you-can-eat buffet)
- Dedicated strategist (this is you, Brad, for now — eventually a senior team member)
- Unlimited revisions
- Priority 24-hour delivery on all requests
- Bi-weekly strategy calls (30 minutes, video)
- Advanced analytics and reporting (custom dashboards, attribution insights)
- Quarterly content strategy review and planning session
- Custom prompt templates built specifically for their brand (they don't share these with other clients)

**What's NOT included:**
- Paid media management (refer to a partner agency)
- Website design/development (refer to a partner)
- Video production (refer to a partner)

**Delivery cadence:** On-demand. Client can request deliverables anytime via Slack or email. Standard turnaround is 24 hours. Rush requests (same-day) are included.

**Your margin at this tier:** ~75-80%. Time investment is roughly 25-30 hours per client per month. The dedicated strategy component is labor-intensive, but at $10K/month per client, 2-3 Scale clients alone is $20-30K/month.

**Why $10,000:** At this level, you're replacing an in-house content team (content manager + copywriter + strategist = $12-18K/month in salary alone). You deliver more, faster, and with better consistency. The unlimited model means they never have to think about content capacity again.

## One-Time Setup Fee

For all new clients, charge a one-time setup fee that covers onboarding and Brand Voice Bible creation.

| Tier | Setup Fee | What It Covers |
|------|-----------|---------------|
| Starter | $1,000 | Client questionnaire, Brand Voice Bible, onboarding process, first content calendar |
| Growth | $2,000 | Everything in Starter + content audit, competitive analysis, 90-day strategy doc |
| Scale | $3,000 | Everything in Growth + custom prompt library, team training session, tool integrations |

**Why charge a setup fee:** It covers your real time investment in onboarding (5-15 hours depending on tier), filters out tire-kickers, and creates immediate cash flow before the first monthly payment hits.

**When to waive it:** Only as a negotiation lever when you're confident the client will stay for 3+ months. Never waive it for Starter clients — they're the most likely to churn.

## Custom Quotes

For anything outside the standard tiers, use this framework:

**Step 1: Calculate your time**
- Estimate hours per month the custom scope would require
- Multiply by your target hourly rate ($150-200/hour for content, $250/hour for strategy)

**Step 2: Compare to tiers**
- Is the custom scope closer to Growth or Scale? Price it accordingly.
- Never price a custom engagement below $3,000/month — it's not worth your time or attention.

**Step 3: Present it as a custom tier**
- Don't call it "custom." Give it a name: "Your [BRAND] Content Partnership" or "[BRAND] Content Engine."
- Include the same structure as a standard tier: deliverables, timeline, check-ins, revisions.

## Pilot Month Pricing

**All new clients start with a pilot month at the Starter rate ($3,000).**

The pilot month is your secret weapon. Here's why:

- **Lower barrier to entry:** A 30-day trial at $3K is easier to say yes to than a 6-month contract at $5K/month.
- **Overdelivery opportunity:** In the pilot month, you deliver 25 pieces instead of 20 and include a bonus strategy document. This makes the value so obvious that upgrading feels like a no-brainer.
- **Proof of concept:** After 30 days, the client has real deliverables they can measure results against. The upgrade conversation becomes "here's what we did at Starter, imagine what we could do at Growth."

**How the pilot converts:**
- Client signs up for pilot month at $3,000 + $1,000 setup fee = $4,000 total
- You overdeliver in month 1
- At the end of month 1, you present the results and recommend a tier
- Target: 80%+ of pilot clients convert to a 3+ month engagement
- Target: 50%+ upgrade from Starter to Growth within the first 90 days

## Pricing Psychology Notes

**Anchoring:** Always present Scale ($10K) first in conversations. When the prospect hears $10K, $5K feels reasonable and $3K feels like a steal. In proposals, list the tiers from highest to lowest.

**Value framing:** Never compare your price to other agencies. Compare it to the cost of hiring in-house:
- Junior copywriter: $4-5K/month (one person, limited output)
- Content manager + copywriter: $8-12K/month (still inconsistent quality)
- Full content team: $15-25K/month (what you replace at Scale tier)

**ROI framing:** For every $1 spent on email marketing, DTC brands see $36-42 in return (DMA/Litmus data). At $3K/month, you only need to generate $3K in attributable revenue to break even — and a single well-written email flow can do that.

**Urgency (use sparingly):** "We're taking on X more clients this month" is fine if it's true. Don't fabricate scarcity. When you genuinely have limited capacity, say so.

## Handling Common Pricing Objections

### "That's more than we expected."
**Response:** "I get it. Most brands are used to paying freelancers $50-100 per piece. But you're not buying individual pieces — you're buying a content system. We build your Brand Voice Bible, create templates specific to your brand, and deliver 20-40 pieces per month that all sound like you. A freelancer at $75/piece for 20 pieces is $1,500/month, but you're also spending 10+ hours managing them, giving feedback, and fixing inconsistencies. We eliminate all of that."

### "Can we start with something smaller?"
**Response:** "The pilot month is exactly that — $3,000 for one month, no long-term commitment. We deliver 20+ pieces, you see the quality and the results, and then we decide together if it makes sense to continue. If it's not a fit after 30 days, no hard feelings."

### "We already have a copywriter/agency."
**Response:** "That's great — most of our clients did too before switching. The difference is speed and consistency. We deliver a full month of content in 15 days using AI-powered systems, and every piece matches your brand voice exactly because it's built from your custom Brand Voice Bible. Happy to do a free audit showing where the current content has gaps — no strings attached."

### "Can you do a discount?"
**Response:** If they're a strong prospect, offer to waive the setup fee instead of discounting the monthly rate. Never discount the monthly rate by more than 10% — it sets a bad precedent and signals that your pricing isn't firm. If they push hard, offer an annual commitment discount: pay for 10 months, get 12 months (17% effective discount).

### "What's the contract length?"
**Response:** "Month-to-month after the pilot. No long-term contracts. We earn your business every month. Most clients stay for 6+ months because the results speak for themselves. If you want to lock in a rate, we offer annual commitments with a built-in discount."

## Discount Framework

| Discount Type | Amount | When to Offer |
|--------------|--------|--------------|
| Setup fee waiver | $1,000-$3,000 | Strong prospect, high likelihood of 3+ month engagement |
| Annual commitment | Pay 10, get 12 (17% off) | Client who wants to commit long-term. Lock them in. |
| Referral credit | $500 off one month | Client refers a brand that signs up. Both parties get the credit. |
| Case study discount | 15% off for 3 months | Client agrees to be a named case study with real metrics. Great for building your portfolio. |

**Never offer:**
- More than 20% off any tier (you'll resent the work)
- Free months (devalues the service)
- Performance-based pricing (you can't control their business outcomes — too many variables)
- Equity or revenue share arrangements (you're a service business, not an investor)

## When to Raise Prices

Review pricing every 6 months. Raise prices when:
- You're at 80%+ capacity (demand exceeds supply)
- Your average client result improves significantly (better results = more value = higher price)
- You add meaningful new capabilities (new content types, new platforms, analytics tools)
- Market rates increase (check competitor agency pricing annually)

**How to raise prices for existing clients:** Grandfather them at their current rate for 60 days, then offer the new rate. Give them the option to lock in the old rate with an annual commitment. Most will accept the increase if you've been delivering results.
